# Heartrow ideas

Everything here is not done yet. Log each idea as one undated line, and mark its status (open, parked, rejected) when it changes. When an idea ships, delete its line, or move one line of evidence to [`history.md`](history.md). Settled design lives in [`design.md`](design.md).

## Open bugs and questions

- Card body overflow safety net (parked): the card body sits at the bottom of a fixed-size card, so a body taller than the card clips its last line. The shortened Cycle and Redraw text fixed the known case (Cycle with Retain and three gains); letting an overlong body grow upward over the art would cover the rest.
- Win check (open): the river shifts as part of End turn, so ending the turn exactly on the goal can lose hearts to an escape and undo the win. It is rare (about every tenth enemy escape, not measured), and the player can avoid it by picking kills or rerolling. Options: keep it and warn near the goal, or check the goal after the reap and before the escape. Measure the bots before changing it. Undecided.
- Level 4 harder since 0.0.9 (open): the designer lost two Standard Level 4 runs (63 and 81 of 96) after clearing it regularly. Suspect the wider effect variety from parameterized effects or the enemy Gold/Power gains; the bot did not see it (`lab-human` is about 1 ♥ better on 0.0.9 at every goal), so it needs more human runs before a balance change.
- Level 4 needs exile and draw (open): every logged Level 4 win exiled 15 to 18 cards and had draw support, and the designer and the bots rarely buy Hearts cards. Try a Tide-heavy and a reaping run to see whether Hearts-first builds can win; the planned effects (Basic conversion, exile bonus, Basic engine) should widen the ways to get rid of Basics or make them worth keeping.
- Undo peeking at random outcomes (open): free undo supports resource planning, but undo after flush, draw, cycle, redraw or take can reveal an alternate random branch without spending the resource. Prefer to keep undo free for solo play; if competitive modes arrive, consider a no-undo or undo-flagged mode instead of making undo a resource.
- Free enemies (open): stacked `weak` makes the discount larger than any enemy cost, so enemies cost 0 and the run can become infinite. `cardCost` floors enemy cost at 0. Options: a floor of 1, a cap on stacking, or keep it as a degenerate case in the preset library. Same question for `cheap` on recruits.
- Cheap enemy alternate rewards (open): a cost-1 enemy can show its minimum 1 ♥ plus an alternate resource even though that exceeds its 1.4 budget. It may be rare enough to keep as a special reward; decide whether to reserve the minimum Heart and suppress the alternate gain.
- A recruit that only draws one card (a 2 ● Draw 1 with nothing else) appears again. An earlier rule said it should not. Decide later.
- The saved run has one slot only; several slots could come with saved generators.
- Bond pricing (parked, feels a little off): Bond costs `0.5` plus `0, 0.7, 1.5` for +1, +2, +3, so +3 costs `2.0` budget, two thirds of a plain +3. A playtest card (4 ●, 1 ⚔, Bond +3 ⚔, Retain) felt cheap, since a Retained Bond card can wait until its type has been played, and a generator with few types makes that likelier. Counter-view: a buyer who plans on several cards of one colour will often miss, and the card's price works out about right against a guaranteed 4 (Bond +3 at 2.0 is 1 + 2 + 0.5 = 3.5 budget for the bonus part, against 3.7 for a cost-4 card). Making it miss would need about 0.9 per level, which may be too harsh. Leave it until more play data; changing it is an engine change, so bump the version then.
- Light theme and colour review (open): the light theme does not look good, and Enemy purple and Tide blue are too close in both themes. Decide whether to keep a light theme (a mockup would settle it) and fix the two colours in one pass over both themes.
- Open question for `draw`, `cycle` and `redraw`: do they need anything in `EffectDef.needs`? They touch no resource.

## Cards and generation

- Attack only with more variety (open, from the designer): the preset has only Foes and Weak, so a run can loop. A Level 2 run got Weak on T3, was stuck at 53 ♥ for a while with only the stronger enemies left in the river, then went "infinite" on T10 and was stopped at 200 ♥ on turn 10 (159 enemies defeated, 98 moves in the last turn). Going infinite is fun, so one mode where it is possible is fine. Since 0.2.0 the preset has Push as a third effect and cards scale by the cards made in a turn (every 7 count as one more turn), so a chain is no longer endless. Still to try: Flip in the preset, a Reload (it is in the pool now, but not in the preset), and a Level 2 replay of the run that went infinite to see how far a chain goes now. The scaling applies to every run; it did not move the bots (the old pool gave `mixed` 43% at goal 48 against 42% before).
- Effect ideas still open (from the designer; each is one effect file and one registry line, in the shape of an existing verb): Hold (the right-edge enemy cannot escape this turn; it could work on any card and is close to the swap-4th-and-5th idea), Rout (+1 ⚔ per enemy defeated this turn, capped at 5 or 10; it only helps Attack only, and is also the "Kin for enemies" idea), Surge (+1 resource per card played this turn, a stronger Kin). Salvage (exile this card for hearts) acts like a keyword such as Retain: more than one keyword on a card costs space, but adds variety; undecided.
- Bots for the new effects (open, the designer wants `lab-human` taught first): `reload`, `push`, `shift` and `echo` are unknown to the bots, which give them the default effect value, and they cost `mixed` about 10 points of hit rate at goal 48 once they joined the Standard pool. Teach the buying policies what they are worth (and when to play Push), or leave the other bots as a measure of a deck that ignores them.
- Exile drought (open, from a 78 of 96 playtest): no Exile card was on offer for the whole run, so several turns were duds. Check how often a Standard run has no Exile in the river, and whether the pool dilution made it worse.
- Attack only loop (open, from playtests): the preset is now Flip, Foes, Bulk, Weak, Push and Shift. A Weak loop is still possible but hard (the designer started one on turn 10 after sacrificing hearts, and a new player may not find it); it needs more playtests. The hearts the designer reached before giving up (when an endless loop felt certain) rose from about 80 with Foes, Weak and Push, to 120 with Flip and Bulk added, to 200 with Shift added, so a bigger pool makes the loop harder. Bots on the new set: `mixed` 35% at goal 48, with a median of 35 and a best of 302. Earlier sets: with only Foes, Weak and Push a Weak card on turn 1 or 2 started a loop of 23 to 41 plays with a discount of −6 to −11; adding `basics`, `hoard` and `echo` as maximum dilution lost a run at about 48 ♥ on turn 20, because escapes cost as much as the deck made. Another option is to cap the loop in the Weak effect itself.
- Echo is rare (open, from a Standard playtest with no Echo seen): in 200 Standard seeds of 24 turns, Echo is 203 of 28800 generated cards, the rarest effect (Bond 405, Hoard 389, Exile 998), so about 40% of runs never see one. Find out why (recruit only, and price 2) and whether to give it a bigger share.
- Echo as an enemy effect (open): Echo repeats an enemy you defeated, but it is a recruit effect only, so it never sits on an enemy. A defeat effect that repeats the last card used would be possible; it has not been designed or tried.
- Beginner-friendly preset (open, from the designer): Moss economy was meant as the beginner challenge where the order of play hardly matters. The Retain changes may have ended that. Keep Echo and other order-sensitive effects out of it, and check whether the preset is still beginner-friendly.
- Effect families with more than one family each (open): an effect has one family today, and the generator list uses it for grouping. Filtering by family later may want several.
- Exile shows up often in Moss (open, from playtests): the designer saw it on many cards after Cheap was removed, and in one run 25 exiles for 24 recruits. A suspected cause is the price: Exile costs 1, and among the Moss effects only Bond (0.5) is cheaper, so a card with an effect budget under 1.5 can roll only Exile or Bond. Not measured yet. To measure: the share of Exile among effect cards in Moss by budget band. Options if confirmed: a higher Exile price, or a minimum budget for it.
- Cost 3 is missing from plain recruits (open, from Simple playtests; engine change, so a version bump): a recruit's cost is fitted from what it spends, `ceil((spent − 0.3) / 0.85)`, and a plain gain is 1 per unit, so 2 units fit cost 2, 3 units jump to cost 4, 4 to 5, 5 to 6. Cost 3 needs a non-integer spend (a Heart is 1.5, an effect has its own price). Simple has no effects and few Hearts, so only 3.0% of its recruits cost 3, against 20.2% at cost 2 (Standard 7.4%; 200,000 cards at Level 4). Any base value leaves one gap, since 1 / 0.85 is more than one cost per unit: a base of 0.3 puts it at 3, 0.5 moves it to 4, 0.6 to 5. Early turns often hold exactly 3 ● and nothing costs 3 (in Simple runs 47 ● and 37 ● were wasted over a run). Options: (1) change `RECRUIT_BUDGET_BASE` to move the gap, but a base of 0.6 makes a cost-3 card give +3 and a cost-4 card +4 (a third stronger), so the slope must be retuned with it and the bots re-measured; (2) a cost-3 draw takes a Heart instead of one unit (+1 ● and +1 ♥ spends 2.5 and fits cost 3), so the gap fills with hearts cards; (3) give the 0.85 left over at cost 3 to something that costs less than a unit, such as Retain at 0.5 (a retained plain card keeps its gold for the next turn); (4) leave the formula and let the Simple starter of 3 ● and 2 ⚔ (a first hand of exactly 3 ● and 2 ⚔) decide whether cost 3 is still missed. Measure the bots first.
- Keywords as a generator knob (open, from the designer): Retain is a keyword, not an effect, yet it is rolled only inside an effect's budget (`make.ts`, the `retain` terms of each effect), so plain cards never get it and Simple only (no effects) has none. A `keywords` list on the generator, next to `types`, `resources` and `effects` (and `kw=` in the link), would let any generator roll Retain on plain recruits too: a plain +2 with Retain spends 2.5 and fits cost 3, which fills the cost-3 gap above. A retained plain card banks its gold for a later turn, a new decision in Simple ("wait for the big buy"). Decided: queued for the next batch of engine changes (a version bump, with the Simple starter), and the chance on plain cards is an internal constant of 10%, not a knob (no other chance is one). Decided: like `types`, `res` and `fx`, a link that leaves out `kw` means all keywords, so Standard has Retain on plain recruits and every preset gets it too (they all have effects today), and an empty `kw=` is a generator with no Retain anywhere, which makes a no-Retain preset possible (the keyword knob also gates the Retain an effect rolls). It gives about 10% of plain recruits Retain and shifts the random streams, so every old seed changes (the version bump and `v=` mark old links). Open: the bots' `retainAware` on plain cards, whether a no-Retain preset is worth having, and what the second keyword would be (Retain is the only one today).
- Basic row recruits: let Basic cards appear in the row as low-complexity purchases with a more generous budget (no `.85` conversion, maybe a small bonus). Decide whether their cost and effect ceiling need separate limits.
- Multi-colour recruits: two-colour and rare rainbow cards whose played colours all count. Candidate budget `C × .85^n + .3`. Decide display, affinities, whether a rainbow card alone satisfies `all3`, and whether `all3` should need three cards instead of three colour tokens.
- Row-discard effect that removes cards matching a category (all cheap cards, enemies you cannot beat, all Ember, the fifth card). Risk: category targeting adds complexity and dilutes the pool. Also a row-targeting effect to reduce dead late offers.
- Effect that draws a temporary sixth card into the row (refill only up to five afterwards).
- Refund part of a recruit's cost when it is exiled.
- Exile bonus: a thin deck leaves cards like a lone Draw 1 with no reason to exile them. Either a card gives a bonus when exiled (refund, heart, draw) or an exile effect pays for any card exiled. Decide this together with the refund idea. Needs an "on exile" hook.
- Exile from the recruit row: weak (rarely more than one per turn) but saves wasted exiles. Does the card go to your exile pile (Vault counts it) or leave the game?
- Type-conversion effect that turns Basic cards into a chosen gem type. Decide whether it is permanent and how it competes with exile.
- Parametrized Basics (open): roll the amount and the card count of `basics` the way `hoard` does, so a Basic-heavy deck can pay more than `+1` per 4. Not done with Hoard; Basics are still fixed at `+1` per 4 basic cards.
- Swap river cards (open): let the player swap the 4th and 5th river card, for a flush or through a card effect (probably with Retain, since it is only useful at the right moment). Motivated by a wanted card that could not be bought with the resources at hand.
- Retain on Cycle (open): neither Draw nor Cycle can roll Retain today. Retain on Draw is pointless, since a Draw card is always played, but a retained Cycle could wait in hand for a bad draw.
- Strip-colour effect (open): the opposite of conversion, it turns a coloured card into a Basic, working like exile. It only helps the Basics effect, so it needs more than that to be worth a card.
- Basic as a generator type (open, daring): let Basic recruits roll in the row (see "Basic row recruits"). It dilutes the pool, but a diluted Standard pool is the aim: players adapt to what they are dealt, and presets and challenges serve those who commit to a style before the game.
- Growing cards whose payout increases after each play. Decide whether growth belongs to the card instance or its definition and how duplication interacts.
- Flush counter as an effect input (gains grow with each flush). Do not store "was stuck" in `GameState`: a human's reason for flushing is not knowable.
- Opening-turn trigger for drafted Basics when no coloured type has been played yet (starter Basics would not carry it).
- Three enemy effect variants that, when defeated, double the selection weight of non-lean effects for future Moss, Ember or Tide recruits for the rest of the run (2× weight, not "+200%").
- Enemy cost minimum of one after discounts (see free enemies).
- Reprice `weak`. Add a `flush` effect (probably enemies only) and a `haste` effect that resolves a recruit's effect when recruited.
- Count recruiting as playing a type. Let draw-only cards count as simple. Cap stacked Retain cards.
- Reward rare runs of identical recruit cards as an achievement; prevent flush farming.
- If Basics are removed, test starter compositions such as 3 Moss / 3 Ember / 3 Tide.
- Parametrised effects (open question): `foes`, `pile`, `all3` and `vault` are fixed text today (`hoard`, `weak` and `cheap` are the model). Possible parameters: the currency, the divisor and the amount. `basics`, `pile` and `vault` could roll amount and divisor through the shared count-gain helper. Each would need a price per step. Generator settings must not change rolled parameters except through the allowed resources and types. Heart payouts for Bond (today it pays only its card's gold or power) and Kin belong here: more ways to win are good even at a low chance, about 15% on Tide cards as with plain gains and about 7% on Moss and Ember, to be tested.
- Smarter Retain for bots (open): `retainChanges` says playing `exile`, `cheap`, `weak`, `flip` and the reaps always changes something, so bots never hold them and Retain on those cards is a pure budget cost for them. Exile could wait until a hand has a card worth exiling, Weak until an enemy is in the row, Cheap until a recruit is buyable, Draw while the deck is small. Retain usefulness by effect, from the designer's play: useful to wait on are Exile, Cycle, Cheap, Weak, Foes, All3, Pile and the reaps (wait for a double or triple), situational are Swap, Bond, Kin, Basics and Coin, and rarely useful are Army, Hoard, Vault, Draw and Redraw (it often breaks Retain plans). Possible shape: one chance per effect by usefulness, flat price 0.5, Exile at 1.
- Retain terms (open): any effect can roll Retain now, with a per-effect price and chance (see design). The bots lost 4 to 6 hit points at goal 48 from the extra budget spent on Retain they rarely use, so the first tuning questions are whether the strong tier should cost 1 or 0.5, and whether bots should buy or hold Retain on Draw and Exile. Original v5 rule, for the record: Retain only on effects with a condition that can fail or wait (foes, all3, vault, pile, kin, bond, army, swap), not on unconditional ones (draw, exile, cheap, weak, coin, basics, reaping, plain gains, enemies); its author agreed it was too narrow for cycle, purge, exile and draw.
- Unified resource leans (open): after the current parametrization work, consider unifying the existing 70/30 effect lean with direct resource selection without changing recruit balance accidentally.
- Coin payout variants (open): compare 50/50 flips such as 1/3 or 3/5 with the current 2/4 payout, including the budget price and how much risk the lower result creates.
- Show calculated values for conditional effects without crowding the card. Mark cards that need a decision so Play simple is less necessary.
- Generator editor warning: recruits are bought with gold, so a generator with recruit types but no gold (no Basic gold and no gold resource) leaves every recruit unbuyable, and the run is only enemies. Warn in the editor, or say so in the summary.
- Generator editor: the Effects fieldset could get search or filtering if the list grows.

## Enemies

- Boss enemies every tenth turn that cost two resources, or bosses that create pressure while they remain in the row. Open: whether board effects expire or persist, and how the player removes them.
- Bosses with two resource tracks and persistent partial payments (pay 3 ● and 4 ⚔ one turn, 4 ● and 6 ⚔ the next). Bosses should not be affected by the normal flush; a special effect could reroll them and the fifth card.
- More enemy variety, and more enemies at higher levels.
- Easier early rounds (open, from a playtest): the first river was 4●, 3⚔, 3●, 2●, 4● ("not even that good"), and the early turns can make or break a run. Bulk enemies now offset the cheap 1 ⚔ for 1 ♥ enemies, so an early Bulk no longer feels guaranteed. Candidates: a lower early cost range, more cheap cards in the first rows, or cheaper early enemies. Measure with the bots before changing anything.
- Bulk tuning (open): at 12 cards a Bulk enemy costs only 1 ⚔ more than normal, which the designer finds barely noticeable, and it is rolled mostly on cheap early enemies because it costs no budget. Check the premium (reference deck size 16, 4 cards per step) and its frequency after playtests.

## Starters, builds and modes

- Simple only starter of 3 ● and 2 ⚔ (open, for the next batch of engine changes, so a version bump): fewer Basics to drown out, perhaps with one extra flush to make up for bad first deals. Four hand runs with 3 ● and 2 ⚔ at the end of 0.2.0: Level 3 won at 64 ♥ (T24), 68 ♥ (T22, 81 ♥ at the end, 4 flushes left) and 65 ♥ (T17, 118 ♥ at the end, 22 cards, 35 kills, no exile, replay in `local/run-simple-0.2-118h.json`), plus one out of turns at 41/64 ♥ with the usual starter. The comfortable runs bought recruits with gold and power, and later with hearts, and kept buying double gains and heart gains. The 118 ♥ run is the first sign that Simple at Level 4 can be won. Bots (in `tools/baseline/bots-current.md`): with 5 Basics Level 2 goes from 4 to 15% to 52 to 74%, Level 3 from 0% to 11 to 29%, and Level 4 stays at 1 to 4%, so the human runs beat the bots at Levels 3 and 4. A hand run with the usual 10 Basics ended out of turns at 76/96 ♥ (27 kills, 16 recruits), so Level 4 with 10 Basics is hard but near (three more runs with 10 Basics ended at 63, 70 and 82 ♥, the last with 37 kills, 14 recruits and no exile), and 5 Basics may make Level 1 too easy: try a start of 5 ● and 3 ⚔ (8 Basics) next.
- Turns per generator (open, from playtests): Moss economy ended 1 to 13 ♥ short of Level 2 in four tries and may be too hard at Level 3 and above with 24 turns, since its engine peaks in the last turns. Each preset could carry its own number of turns per goal, or a turn bonus. By the reframe rule this is a difference in numbers, so a plain field on the preset and on the custom generator (and its link and editor), not a branch on the preset id. Today `standardMode` fixes 24 turns for every level.
- Moss effect set (open, from a playtest): a custom Moss set of only Draw, Exile, Kin, Cycle and ReapG won Level 2 at 85 ♥ on turn 19, against 57, 51, 60 and 63 ♥ out of turns for Moss economy. A second run without Flip and Cheap won Level 2 at 88 ♥ on turn 20. `smart` at goal 48 (40,000 games each): Moss economy 6.4%, without Flip 5.4%, without Cheap 9.0%, without both 10.9%, so Cheap is the weak effect for the bot and Flip alone does not matter (details in `tools/baseline/bots-current.md`); the human results are larger than the bot's, since `smart` never seeks ReapG. Cheap is out of the Moss preset since 0.2.0, though the designer found it useful early on the older build: a −1 Cheap card allowed two double purchases in a Level 4 win, so the removal may be worth revisiting. Still open: remove Flip as well (it looked neutral for the bot), add Cycle, or remove Bond and other effects that pay no hearts. A Hoard that pays hearts or a Vault would overlap with Tide hearts, which is built on Vault. Note that `smart` has no Cycle policy.
- Starter builds: begin with a small synergistic deck or a turn-10 setup. Decide whether the player chooses or gets one at random. Also two experimental formats: constrained generators (tests viability) and curated starter decks (tests whether starting with the build is fun).
- Let challenges shape the early row, for example a run with no Ember, without a permanent starter deck.
- Build Lab: give the player a budget and let them assemble cards under the generator's limits. Keep it separate from normal scores.
- Paid starter builds: stronger packages spend starting hearts (`1.5` budget per heart), so the run begins in debt. The tested 12-card Moss package costs `27.5` raw budget (about −19 ♥); a −40 ♥ charge tests a 2× premium. A candidate Moss starter: 2× `+3 ●`, 2× Coin, 2× Kin, 1× Army, 2× retained ReapG, 3× retained Swap (`29.5` raw, about −20 ♥).
- Starter shape in the generator: starting with no Basics at all, or a tiny starter (a couple of 1 ● cards) for a harder Pacifist.
- Level-specific goal variants. Distinct modes with separate high-score tables. Objective modes (defeat M enemies, reap 15 hearts, exile every Basic, hold 12 cards), possibly as a campaign.
- Curated card pools or expansion sets instead of one diluted random pool.
- Endless continuation after the normal run (goals, costs, escapes, scoreboards).
- Long Run could take a goal of its own, so `goal` would apply to every mode.
- Saving generators: named slots in localStorage, with a Save button next to the preset list, only for Custom settings so built-in presets cannot be overwritten. Names stay in the browser and show as plain text, so no swearword filter is needed. If names ever travel in links or a public list, cap their length and show them as text only.
- Starter ratio benchmark (open): compare the default 6G / 4X starter with a gold-heavier 7G / 3X starter using the same bot seeds, goals, purchases, escapes and final hearts before changing Standard.
- Moss economy as the beginner preset (open): one human run won Level 3 with it and found few decisions per turn. Repeat it a few times before promoting it, and decide where a beginner would find it (a "Start here" entry, or the first preset in the editor list).
- Moss economy with `cycle` and `redraw` (open): a human run won with 61 ♥ on turn 24 but had no cycle to thin 4 exiles. Adding both lowers the draw share of a new card from about 4.9% to 2.8%, because the effect pool is split over more effects. Test whether to add them, or add only `cycle`, with bot runs before changing the preset.
- Real preset descriptions (today they are generated). Pacifist note: with 10 Basic gold cards you are guaranteed 5 ● every turn, so the run feels easy.
- Bots for challenge balancing once challenge definitions and playable presets exist.

## Build hypotheses to validate

Candidate archetypes, not approved balance targets. Test constrained generators first to see whether each concept works when its enablers are available; test starter decks separately.

- Tide heart engine: draw, discard, Retain, exile and direct hearts (a Tide-heavy run has won).
- Exile vault: exile Basics and use `+1 ♥ per 3 cards in exile`. Depends on exile availability.
- Ember combat: power, `foes`, `weak`, discard-pile power and enemy rewards. Pure Ember has one Level 4 win; repeat it before treating it as reliable.
- Simple only, a dilution strategy (untested): with no thinning, drawing or cycling, a player won by keeping the deck thin by buying only cards better than any owned. The alternative is to drown the 10 Basics in cheap `+2 ●` and `+2 ♥` cards after securing 4 to 6 damage per turn for escapes. Try it by hand, then with a bot rule, and compare with the thin-deck run.
- Moss economy: `flip`, Basic-count gold, gold Reaping, expensive recruits, Swap as an outlet. Pure Moss is unproven.
- Swap converter: generate one resource and exchange it. Probably a bridge, not an archetype.
- Discard-pile engine: cycle or redraw to grow the discard, then `+1 ⚔ per 3 cards in discard`. An Ember/Tide hybrid.
- Wide tribal deck: many cards with `army`, `kin` or Bond. The counterexample to "thin is always best".
- Basic engine: keep Basics to power Basic-count effects, through duplication and Hoarder payoffs, so Basics are a positive choice and not an early-exile target.
- Rainbow combo and `all3` build: play all three types with Bond, Kin and `all3`, and collect `all3` cards across colours without making a generic best strategy.
- Reaping or greed: make excess resources and convert the rest to hearts at end of turn. Probably a finishing engine.
- Micro-deck: four or five efficient cards. Does consistency make up for lost flexibility?
- Draw engine: enough Draw, cycle, redraw and Retain for reliable resources. Possibly shared by several colours.
- Bot outlier analysis (open): report the best run's seed and replayable context, then compare its sampled decisions with the greedy run on the same game to learn when randomness finds a stronger line.
- Late flush (open): remove the generic final-two-turn flush veto because late costs are scaled up; compare named greedy bots and restore the veto only if the extra flushes reduce hearts.
- Human bot readiness rules (open): estimate current-hand gold with Coin worth 3 expected gold, deprioritise early Reaping but reconsider ReapG and ReapP as late Heart candidates, require setup for Bond, Army, Kin, all3, Pile and Vault, and use one effect-value policy instead of one permanent policy per effect.
- Bot flush resource gate (open): do not flush when all remaining resources are below the expected current card cost, even when an affordable row card is later vetoed by the buying policy.

## Screens, modes and sharing

- Home screen: a plain first version is built (see the design doc). Still open: its look (the title screen concepts below), the look of the level picker, and a Challenge entry.
- Challenge screen polish (open): the first version is built (see the design doc). The mouse-drag flick, click to centre and touch feel are checked and fine. Still to check by hand: the layout on a wide desktop screen, and the dots with many challenges (they shrink to fit).
- Standard screen for newcomers (open): the Standard screen with Long Run is built. Still open: a new player sees only one or two difficulties (perhaps a tutorial and 48 ♥) and the rest locked, which gives something to look forward to. It builds on Sequential Standard unlocks.
- Generated card art for the challenge cards (parked): the icons are hand-drawn paths, and Simple, Pacifist and Custom have the weakest ones. Leave them unless they bother a player.
- Maximum hearts as a challenge (open): show or reward the most hearts a run reached beyond the goal, for example a score tier per goal. A win ends the run at the goal turn, so the final hearts are already the score.
- Goals as data, next uses (open, from the designer): a goal is a record with turns and difficulty steps, so another scaling is a new record, for example an easier ramp for Attack only (unconfirmed suspicion: enemies outscale the player after about turn 10, so a run that is not ahead early is lost). Also which presets a goal can or cannot apply to, and the Long Run target of 200 ♥ (a first guess) to balance against human runs.
- Stars as a charm-shop currency (parked, premature): the per-goal results of the Challenge screen can be drawn as stars and later be spent on charms.
- Simple preset with Exile (open): allow Exile in the beginner card pool as a deliberate exception to “Simple only,” then consider renaming it to “Simple start” or “Plain cards.”
- Sequential Standard unlocks (open): keep Level 1 available, unlock each later level after the player wins the previous one, and show locked buttons such as “Win Level 1 to unlock.”
- Level names (open): "Easy, Medium, Hard" for Levels 1 to 3 wait for evidence, since the designer struggled with Level 1 early on yet has won Level 3 twice. Revisit with feedback from new players.
- Challenge: the player chooses a generator and tries to make it work. Custom challenges choose or ban types and effects, with several slots to save, edit and duplicate. Start with the Level 1–3 goals. Leave starter decks out of the first iteration.
- Waste-nothing challenge for Moss economy (open, from a playtest): a goal such as "win without wasting gold" or "waste under N". In that preset gold is the only currency, the designer wastes about 2 gold per turn, and undoing to take 2 less would avoid it, so it is the preset where a low-waste goal is doable, while zero may be too hard. Same spirit as the objectives below; the run summary already shows wasted gold and power, and a second record per goal would hold the lowest waste.
- Goal modifiers (open, from the designer, too soon for this batch): an objective such as no escapes or under N wasted is meaningless alone (play nothing and waste nothing), so it should sit on top of a target, for example "no escapes on 48" or "no escapes on 64", maybe several together. Open points: how the goal list shows them (a toggle row under the level, or chips), whether each combination is its own result record, and the reward (one star for the target and a mark for each modifier, or one star for everything).
- Challenge objectives (open): finish a run with no escapes, or with fewer than 10 wasted Gold and fewer than 10 wasted Power; recognize that passing on a card can be correct when it leads to no useful river action.
- Show the mode, difficulty, goal, seed and run brief before a menu-launched game and in an overview popup. A shared link starts the game directly and opens the overview once. Compose the brief from structured selections, with only a short length-limited player note.
- Standard setup with an editable seed field.
- Keep the game screen mostly unchanged: a route back to the menu and access to the overview.
- Compendium (open, from the designer): a mobile-friendly reference that lists every effect, resource and card type with plain-language descriptions, ideally with an animation per effect, so the short card text ("Echo the last card played or enemy defeated") can stay short. It links the generator instead of pretending the procedural pool is a fixed card set.
- Use the View Transitions API for screen changes, with the screen sliding over an unmoving background; respect reduced motion.
- Visual mockups first (blue, white, pink, buttons, fields, chips, modals); they need no interaction. Sketch code is on `feat/game_screens_sketch`, mockups in `local/mockups/`.
- Title screen: the risograph concept is the best candidate for now, flat (no gradient button borders), in both themes. A URL switch could show flat cards or in-game cards. Use `text-wrap: balance` for button subtitles, a slightly bigger heart that keeps the A readable, no hover flicker, a bit of fidget on the home screen, and menu buttons at the bottom on phones. Next test: a second screen in the same style (the generator editor or the game screen).

- Keep the current result screen unless mockups show a problem.

## Interface and presentation

- Tint the play screen with the mode's colour (tried and reverted): the game screen's background was the meta button's 16% mix of the accent and the theme background. It suits some modes, but the buttons and other UI elements do not fit with it in others, so every surface would need the same tint (panels, buttons, cards) before it can ship.
- Gate and River naming (open, built as a trial): the fifth slot is "Gate" with a label, a lock on the ring and chevrons in the River bar; the designer is not sure it is clear that the River is the whole row and the Gate its last slot. Open: "Gate" is a placeholder for lack of better ("Border" and "Edge" were the other candidates), the lock may go, the icon could be a danger sign (a clock is taken by Retain), "River" clashes with poker (the fifth card is the river, the fourth the turn), and code and docs still say "right edge".
- Huge hands on phones (open): the staggered two-row hand (from 10 cards, `.hand.large`) stays playable up to about 17 cards (9 in one row, 8 in the other). A Draw and Exile engine reached hands of 21 to 27 cards (a 240 ♥ Standard run, 414 moves), which cannot be handled on a phone. Options, in the designer's order of preference: fix the layout so there is no cap (stack or scroll the hand; roguelikes are for breaking the game), a play-all-of-one-kind shortcut, a hard hand cap at 17 (Gemblades caps at 12 and players criticise it for limiting the build), stop generating Draw cards once the deck has 5, or leave it as the reward of a broken run. Prove 17 on a phone first.
- Draw has no downside (open): in a 240 ♥ run, 11 Draw cards gave hands of 20 or more cards every turn, and nothing pushes the player to exile Draw again or stop buying it. Ideas to test: a different Draw price curve, a diminishing return, or a cap on cards drawn per turn. Probably an outlier, so first collect more runs with 8 or more Draw cards.
- Shift is rare on recruits (open): Shift, Vault, Hoard and Echo each make about 2% of recruit effects, against 4 to 6% for most others, because colour-leaning effects get extra copies in the pool. Shift is 8% of enemy effects, so players meet it mostly on enemies. Decide if Shift should lean to a colour or get an extra weight on recruits.
- Flight versus box change (open): a flight shows what the card produced, but a Swap in the same play changes the box by something else (6 ⚔ flies in and the box moves by 1). The swap pulse also plays before the flight lands, so the box bounces twice. Show the swap after the flight, or tie the flight amount to the box change.
- Font coverage (open): the page font has none of ● ⚔ ♥, so they come from a fallback font and can differ per system. A font or icon set with these glyphs would make the symbols consistent, and would allow arrows in the log.
- Better replay controls (open): holding "Next move" or "Undo" now repeats, slowly at first and then faster. Still open: a jump to a move number or turn, or a run-to-end-of-turn button.
- Arrow-key navigation and controller support (open, later): the page is one list of 19 to 50 buttons, so a keyboard player has to Tab a long way. Arrow keys should move between the zones (river, hand, buttons) and between cards. A controller would follow from this, because a browser reports a pad through the Gamepad API, and an Android WebView sends a D-pad and the A button as arrow and Enter key events. Enter does not ramp the replay hold like the mouse does (the OS sets its repeat rate); it could start the same hold on key down and stop it on key up.
- Button row in the middle, status at the bottom (open): the action buttons move when the status line changes length or the hand wraps to a second row, which makes holding a button awkward. Putting the buttons between the river and the hand, with the status last, is the classic deckbuilder layout and keeps them in place.
- Stats bar restyle: coloured chips with an icon, a big number and a small label, with Flushs and Piles styled like the other buttons. Draw it in the style of today's cards (rounded corners, header strips, pill badges), without riso hard shadows. Keep the fluid mobile-like layout up to iPad portrait; no separate desktop layout today.
- Riso game screen edits (for later): on desktop, bundle all stats into one bordered panel with Flushs and Piles outside it; drop the border around the hand; too many borders overall. On phones the cards are squeezed and the transparency on real-style cards looks bad.
- Mockup lessons: keep the real card ratio (118 × 175), round the inner border corners, never let descriptions overlap the border, brief mockup agents with the real card CSS and not a style name. Skip exile needs a place on narrow screens.
- Alternative phone skin: bigger card text without effect descriptions, with keyword meanings in a help section or on long press. Styling is swappable on the web, so it can be a skin.
- Draw cards even larger and shrink them more, and test large system text on a phone.
- Animation ideas, all optional: overlap recruiting, refilling the river, exile and the hand closing up; a shuffle animation counting the piles box (0 / 9 to 9 / 0); the piles number changing colour on a pulse; a new hand dealing while the old one flies away; a "−2 ●" flying out of the box on a spend; the status line changing on the click and not mid-animation.
- Defeated tally as a visible count (add it when the first effect uses it).
- Try six recruit slots if the phone layout holds. Make card art respond to card properties (a rectangle for power, a ring for draw).
- Lovecraftian visual direction as an optional theme (organic Moss and Tide, arcane Ember, horrors, hearts as sanity). A visual and naming direction, not a rules change.
- Optional tutorial with a way to replay it. Sound, transitions and Android packaging.

## Platforms and architecture

- Terminal platform: an ASCII renderer and a CLI with `--seed`, `--moves` and `--moves-file`, so agents can playtest and replay a game headless. It would print events as log lines.
- Capacitor (an Android wrapper around the static web build) is far away, but honour it now: a static relative-path build with no server, the font bundled locally (done), touch-first UI with safe-area insets and no hover-only affordances, no essential keyboard shortcuts, and persistence behind a small `Storage` interface (`get`, `set`, `list`, `remove`) so game logic never calls `localStorage`. Android back, pause and resume are platform concerns; core state is serialisable, so the web layer can autosave on pause. Defer app id, icons, splash, signing and the store listing until a one-afternoon spike.
- TIC-80 (maybe never): core stays small, plain data and free of ambient APIs, which `check-portability` already enforces. It would need a `platform/tic80` adapter, a bundle script (`vp pack` with `target: es2020`) and a size check. Platforms would declare capabilities (`saveDecks`, `deckBuilder`, `animations`, `themes`) and the menus would hide what a platform lacks.
- A meta layer in core: a second reducer (`core/app`) for the current screen, mode selection, saved decks and history, plus pure view-model selectors per screen (`runView(state)`, `deckBuilderView(app)`), so the terminal platform can reuse the navigation. The builder's rules (budget, heart debt, validation) would be core and tested; its drag and drop is web only.
- Decks as data: a versioned JSON document (`{ v, name, cards, startHearts }`) validated against the paid-starter rule, with import and export as a pasteable text blob. A second packed bit format would fit a few decks in TIC-80's 1024 bytes and double as a short share code.
- Themes versus card skins (web only): a theme is colour tokens switched with `data-theme` (light, dark, later Lovecraft); a skin is card markup and CSS fed by the same view-model. Settings (theme, skin, reduced motion, animation speed) belong to the web platform's persisted settings, not to the game state.
- Daily seeded runs need only a seed source.
- Stronger bots beyond hand-written policies: tune policy weights by self-play (random search or hill climbing on weight multipliers), starting from the existing one-ply and rollout `lookahead`. Pick by weight (varied, good for statistics) versus the maximum with some noise (better for strength comparisons); maybe both as options on `pickMove`.

## Naming

The working title stays Heartrow until something clearly better turns up. The repo name and the game name do not have to match.

- Heartrow is unique and easy to search on itch.io, but Google autocorrects it to Heathrow, and it keeps the word "row", which the owner dislikes. "Ascension" and "heart row" each match over 1000 itch.io projects.
- A coined name can become a genre label (Balatro), but invented words like Ebbrow are disliked. The owner likes Mossfall, but it ties the brand to Moss, which may not survive a Lovecraft reskin.
- Not checked for any candidate: Google Play, trademark registers (EUIPO, USPTO, card-game classes 9 and 28), and a manual itch.io search for names other than Heartrow.
- Heartwrack is the best alternative (wrack means seaweed and wreckage, so it covers Tide and Moss and suits Lovecraft; no "row"; the web autocorrects it to Heartwork and Heartworm). Hollow Hearts fits the sanity reskin but is generic. Edgefall survives a reskin but is taken by a geometry-style game. Headwater, Millrace and Weir are river images without "row", unchecked; Weir is hard to search for.
- Rejected: Ebbrow (reads as invented), Undertow (an existing tide-based roguelite deckbuilder), Tollrow (keeps "row"), Heartfall, Hearthfall and Heartdeep (crowded).
- Open: does the Lovecraftian reskin rename the project? The river image fits the flow and the Tide colour but is hard to make work visually with cards.
- Genre vocabulary: Ascension's shared line is the centre row (six cards) and Star Realms calls it the Trade Row (five). "River" is a poker term and not the standard deckbuilder word. Ascension has no browser version.
- Repo decisions still open: a licence (the owner's other repos use MIT) and whether the repo stays private until the game is presentable.

## Statistics and progression

- Unplayed and discarded cards as run statistics (open, from a playtest): the "wasted" figures count unspent resources, so a player who avoids waste by not playing a gold card loses the gold without the summary seeing it. Candidates: cards left unplayed at the end of turns (retained ones counted apart), and cards discarded by Cycle. The same split tells apart a stuck hand from a held one. Core change, no gameplay effect, like the heart sources.
- A stronger run clears the easier levels (open, tried and removed before 0.2.0, needs more thought, from the designer): a run that reaches the target of an easier level of the same length and scaling would win that level too, as a gift to the player. In a trial it made a mess: the cleared level had to store its own target as the hearts and the last turn of the run as the fastest turn, so its numbers looked low or odd, and giving the run's hearts to every level looked wrong. Playing the levels one by one is simpler to reason about ("won L1 with 65? Confirm it at L2"), and it leaves room for different scalings per level. A star without a number is one alternative.
- One rating instead of four separate goals (open, undecided, from the designer, to think over): the four levels as one score rating with one to four stars, in the style of typical game performance ratings. Today the levels stay four separate goals, and a stronger run clears the easier ones. The "fastest win" turn of a goal cleared this way is the last turn of the run, not the turn its target was first met.
- Biggest turn as a run statistic (open, from a playtest): the most hearts gained in one turn, with its turn number, for example 26 ♥ on the last turn of a Moss run after a few retains. It needs a per-turn heart counter in the run stats, and it could be a third record per goal.
- Results as a link (open, small): a "Copy link" for the results that puts the record in the URL hash, so it can be messaged and tapped on another device. It only opens on one origin and puts results in the browser history.

- Run summary as a proper screen (open): the summary dialog and log lines are the first version (moves, heart sources). Next: a nicer layout (a bar or list per heart source), and further statistics the player now counts by hand.
- Show the stored game version of a result (open): each result already stores the version of the run that last changed it. A later screen can say "won on 0.1.0", and an optional "beat it again on this version" badge is an open question; old wins keep counting either way.
- Contribution statistics for gold and power (open): hearts by source are done (`stats.heartsFrom`). The same for gold and power would show what paid for each purchase and how much was wasted by source. Decide grouping by definition or instance (today it is by effect id).
- Between-run progression loop (open): add collectible out-of-run resources, such as gems, from completed runs and let players buy and equip limited lucky charms that alter the generator, for example making Draw twice as likely; decide whether rewards come from wins, final hearts, kills, or a capped combination, and protect core balance with slots and trade-offs.
- Bots as teachers and rivals (open, from the designer, not fleshed out): spend goal stars on a bot that teaches one strategy (for example a ReapG engine), or treat bots as NPC rivals on a shared seed ("this bot got N ♥ in M turns, can you beat it?"). This would give the strategy bots a reason to exist in the game and not only in the simulator. It needs the strategy bots, a replay shown as a lesson, and the stars-as-currency idea above.
- Achievements, run statistics, cloud support, leaderboards, unlockable effects and progression after the core run is stable.

## Bots

- Effect ablation (open, from the designer): take the effects out of the pool one at a time and measure the hit rate, to learn which are strong and which are weak, then act on the extremes. `npm run sim -- --preset <id> --effects <list>` replaces the effects of a preset, so each run is one command. A bot that does not value an effect (`smart` has no Cycle policy) understates it, so read the result as a lower bound, and try it with `lab-human` too. The designer does not trust ablation numbers from the present bots, since there are too few strategies; strategy bots (below) should come first, and it only costs time to try it meanwhile.
- Strategy bots (open, from the designer): bots that each follow one strategy (a ReapG engine, an exile-thin deck, a Vault deck, a plain attacker), so that the effect ablation and the preset difficulty can be read from the bot that suits them. `smartBuy` scores every card alone (+8 for any effect, no count of copies owned), so it can buy a third ReapG but never seeks one. The Moss presets reward ReapG timing, Retain and Draw, which no bot plays on purpose. Start with a Moss ReapG bot, as the preset with the clearest strategy. Source material: the designer's good Moss runs, kept only when they are good (not every run), to extract what to buy early and what to buy late.
- Bot that plays like the designer's Moss run (open, later): a replay of a 65 ♥ win on turn 18 is kept in `local/run-moss-0.2-t18.json`. Analyse it to derive the strategy as policy rules: the buying order (+2 ♥ plain gains when nothing else is there, one early ReapG, then ReapG with Retain only, Draw, Exile, then gold that raises the average), zero waste, holding Retain ReapG until several are in hand, and exiling hard. Compare the bot against this run with `--explain`. It is the first concrete target for the strategy bots below, and for the bots-as-rivals idea.
- `smart` has no Cycle or Redraw policy (open): it uses `playsFirst`, `retainAware`, `drawFirst`, `exileChoices` and `smartBuy`, which gives every effect a flat +8 value. It was never tuned for Moss economy or Pacifist, so its numbers on those presets are a coincidence and not a benchmark. A Moss or Pacifist bot would need to value Cycle, Redraw, Reap and Bond itself.
- Human bot cycle readiness (open): refine the expected-resource comparison with effect budgets as a small value prior; direct gains from the Cycle/Redraw card now count, and the estimate still needs to cover the draw pile and discard reshuffle.
- Gold and power balance (open): a recruit's main currency follows its colour (Ember always power, Moss always gold, Tide half and half), so a deck heavy in one colour can be short of the other and wastes what it has. Look at whether Standard needs a gold-or-power correction, using bot data over many seeds.
- Better bot (open): combine `mixed` buying with `smartFlush` as a new profile (not a change to `mixed`, which is the v7 baseline). Evidence is in the history file.
- Tutorial mode (open): a predefined seed with a status bar that explains more, as the way in for new players, since Standard is where they will go first and the Moss economy and Ember combat presets have fewer or more decisions than it does.
- Bot tuning (a thread of its own): the bots are very deterministic, with policy weights in the millions. Look at softer weights or sampling, and at teaching them goals above 48, where they hit 0 to 4%.
- Replace the huge and tiny magnitudes (1e12, 1e-9) with human-sized numbers (about 1 to 100) and accept a different move about 1 time in 100. Measure first, change, compare, then judge. Priority tiers (a tier plus a weight within it) are a cleaner alternative.
- Make the bot flush memory pure without a "stuck" flag in `GameState`: a flush counter in state, or the driver passing memory in.
