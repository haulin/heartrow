# Heartrow history

Finished work and evidence, kept for lookup. Do not read it before a change. Current rules are in [`design.md`](design.md) and open work is in [`ideas.md`](ideas.md). Add a line only when it holds evidence a commit message cannot (numbers from a playtest, a reason that was hard to find).

## Bot policies

- Deck-average buying did not beat phased buying, so do not retry it. The experiment bought only recruits that raised the deck's average Gold or Power per card by a minimum uplift (10%, later 9%, 8% and 6%), alone (`lab-human-curve`), as a strict veto on `phaseBuy` (`lab-human-hybrid`), and multiplied with `phaseBuy` (`lab-human-combined`). On 400 greedy goal-48 games with seed 1, `lab-human` reached 54% with 44.11 mean Hearts, and the average-based variants reached 44–51% with 41.2–43.3 mean Hearts. Lowering the threshold from 10% to 6% helped the averages bots, but never reached `lab-human`.
- Deck-average buying on 100 fresh base seeds × 400 games, greedy, at the 6% threshold: `lab-human` hit 53% with 43.7 mean Hearts at goal 48 and 15% with 47.7 at goal 64; `lab-human-curve` 50% with 43.2 and 13% with 46.9; `lab-human-combined` 51% with 43.3 and 13% with 47.0. P10 (30) and P90 (52 and 64–65) matched across the bots, so the gap is in the mean only. The seed-1 worst (7 ♥) and best (78 ♥) runs of the combined bot were seed luck.
- Redraw and end turn only count takes the buying policies would make: before this, a legal take that `phaseBuy` vetoed kept the bot from ending its turn and let it redraw with too few resources. The refactor alone changed nothing (seed 1, 400 games: identical for `mixed`, `build`, `smart` and `lab-human`, and matching the earlier seed-1 sampled snapshot). With the buying check, 100 base seeds (1000–1099) × 400 greedy goal-48 games: `lab-human` 53% → 57% hits (43.7 → 44.3 mean ♥), `mixed` 47% → 47% (42.0 → 42.1), `build` 42% → 43% (41.0 → 41.1), `smart` unchanged at 23% (35.9).
- Hoard and Bulk (0.0.10), 100 seeds × 400 greedy games (1000–1099), hits and mean ♥ at goals 48 / 64, 0.0.9 → both effects: `lab-human` 58% / 44.6, 21% / 49.7 → 55% / 44.0, 18% / 48.4; `mixed` 48% / 42.1, 13% / 45.7 → 46% / 41.8, 12% / 45.1; `build` 43% / 41.3 → 41% / 40.8; `smart` 23% / 35.9 → 21% / 35.2. Hoard alone, with Bulk taken out of the pool, helped slightly (`lab-human` 59% / 44.8 and 22% / 50.0, `mixed` 50% / 42.5 and 15% / 46.5), so Bulk costs the bots about 4 hit points and 0.8 to 1.4 ♥, as expected for a thin-deck counter. Bulk reaches 6.7% of enemies, mostly cheap early ones, because it costs no budget and is the only effect small enough for them. Variety at 2,000,000 samples: 9,695 recruits and 518 enemies observed.
- Weak and Cheap amounts (0.0.10), same setup, hits and mean ♥ at goals 48 / 64 / 80 before → after: `lab-human` 55% / 44.0, 18% / 48.4, 3% / 49.3 → 54% / 43.8, 16% / 48.0, 3% / 49.0; `mixed` 46% / 41.8, 12% / 45.1, 1% / 45.7 → 45% / 41.6, 11% / 44.9, 1% / 45.3; `build` 41% / 40.8, 9% / 43.6 → 40% / 40.7, 9% / 43.3; `smart` 21% / 35.2, 2% / 36.2 → 20% / 35.1, 2% / 36.0. About one hit point lower, since a −2 card is a stronger turn that no bot plans for. Variety at 2,000,000 samples: 9,758 recruits and 555 enemies observed.
- Retain on every effect (0.1.0), same setup, hits and mean ♥ at goals 48 / 64 / 80 before → after: `lab-human` 54% / 43.8, 16% / 48.0, 3% / 49.0 → 48% / 42.6, 13% / 46.2, 2% / 46.8; `mixed` 45% / 41.6, 11% / 44.9, 1% / 45.3 → 41% / 40.3, 9% / 43.1, 1% / 43.5; `build` 40% / 40.7, 9% / 43.3, 1% / 43.7 → 35% / 39.3, 7% / 41.5, 1% / 41.8; `smart` 20% / 35.1, 2% / 36.0 → 16% / 33.3, 2% / 34.0. Retain share of effect recruits went from 36.3% to 34.5% (19.9% to 18.9% of all recruits), so the drop is not from more Retain. It comes from the price: the strong tier (Exile, Draw, Cycle, Purge, Cheap, Weak) pays 1.0 budget instead of 0.5, and the bots rarely gain from waiting on those cards. Variety at 2,000,000 samples: 11,864 recruits and 558 enemies observed.
- Retain price experiments (0.1.0), goal 48 hit rate for `lab-human` / `mixed` / `build` / `smart`, same setup: strong-tier Retain off 55% / 46% / 41% / 21% (the old baseline), at price 0.5 51% / 43% / 38% / 17%, at price 1.0 48% / 41% / 35% / 16%. The loss is linear in the price, because `retainChanges` says playing Exile, Cheap, Coin and the reaps always changes something, so bots never hold them and the budget goes to nothing. Then Retain became one chance per effect by usefulness (0.5, 0.3 or 0.1) at a flat 0.5 price, and `retainAware` holds a Retain Weak card until an enemy is in the row: `lab-human` 52% / 43.4, 15% / 47.2 at goals 48 / 64; `mixed` 42% / 41.0, 10% / 44.0; `build` 38% / 40.0, 8% / 42.4; `smart` 17% / 33.5, 2% / 34.3. That recovers about half of the loss and the rest is the 0.5 price on cards bots cannot use. Variety: 11,872 recruits and 561 enemies observed.
- Ember combat with Hoard and Bulk (0.2.0), 100 seeds × 400 greedy games (1000–1099), goal 48 hit and mean ♥, 11 effects → 13: `lab-human` 78% / 47.8 → 73% / 47.2, `mixed` 90% / 49.2 → 87% / 49.0, `smart` 70% / 46.7 → 64% / 45.8. A few points lower, from the longer pool (the old effects appear 11/13 as often) and from Bulk, which no bot plans for; Ember stays the easiest preset for the bots. Moss economy without Cheap is the "without Cheap" row of `tools/baseline/bots-current.md` (`smart` 9.0%, up from 6.4%). The Purge effect became Redraw in the same version; the pool order is unchanged, so no card or seed moved.
- Long Run as a goal with a 200 ♥ target (0.2.0), 200 games × 10 seeds (1000–1009), greedy, 42 turns: hit rate and mean ♥ for `lab-human` 5% / 117.5 (P90 180), `mixed` 1% / 94.5 (149), `build` 1% / 90.3 (145), `smart` 0% / 62.6 (108). The hearts are the same as before the change, since only the status at the end differs.
- Bot lab ladder (private notes, now removed): builds a benchmark bot one policy at a time. At goal 64 with 20 base seeds × 400 games, greedy `lab-random` 25.6 mean ♥, `lab-play` 25.6 (greedy ties follow legal-move order, so a flat product equals random order), `lab-retain` 27.7, `lab-draw` 27.8. Sampled with `samplePower: 2`: −9.3, 4.7, 5.6, 5.7. So the first policy (play first) is the big step and Retain and Draw add small gains.
- Normalizing policy factors to the 1–10 range did not weaken greedy bots (100 seeds, goal 48 mean ♥, normalized against the old large values): `mixed` 36.6 vs 36.2, `build` 35.6 vs 35.3, `smart` 34.8 vs 34.0, `rush` 17.5 vs 15.5. The large sampled drop came from sampling, not from the factors. A disjoint seed window (1001–1100) moved means by at most 0.2 ♥ and hit rates by at most 1 point; best scores are unstable (`smart` Long Run 245 vs 224), so judge bots by the mean, not the best run.
- Starter 7G / 3X instead of 6G / 4X (100 seeds, greedy, goal 48): `mixed` 36.6 → 38.3 ♥ and 25% → 30% hits, `build` 35.6 → 37.2, `smart` 34.8 → 35.9; `none` and `rush` fell because they stop buying. Promising, not adopted; the Standard starter stayed at 6G / 4X.
- `lab-human` phase work (100 seeds, greedy): the first phased bot averaged 33.1 ♥ at goal 48, below `mixed` at 36.6, because it bought more cards than it could convert. Thresholds `6/16` (early phase ends after turn 6, late starts at turn 16) gave 37.1 ♥ and 26% hits, `8/18` 36.5 and 23%, `10/20` 35.6 and 21%, `6/18` 37.1 and 26%, `6/20` 36.9 and 25%, so `6/16` is the default. A broad "redraw only with more than the average card cost" rule cut `lab-human` to 28.3 ♥ because it also beat affordable cheap recruits; the working rule boosts End turn only when nothing is affordable (37.8 ♥ and 29% hits at goal 48), and the low-resource End turn factor of 4 versus 1.5 made no difference in greedy play, so it is 1.5.
- `lab-human` exiles by deck average (`exileByAverage`): a plain hand card (weak and Foes count as 1 Power) is exiled when doing so lowers the deck's average Gold, Power or Hearts per card by less than 10%; played cards are preferred, unplayed ones count from turn 4. The earlier rule exiled only gold Basics. Threshold sweep on seeds 1000–1099 × 400 greedy goal-48 games (control 57% hits, 44.3 ♥): 5% 54% / 43.8, 10% 58% / 44.4, 15% 57% / 44.2, 25% 55% / 43.9, 35% 54% / 43.7, 50% 53% / 43.5. A fresh window (2000–2099) confirmed 10% over the control: goal 48 56% → 57% hits (44.3 → 44.4 ♥), goal 64 17% → 20% (48.9 → 49.3). It exiles more (5.5 against 3.4 per game) and holds about 1.6 fewer cards. The gain is small, within about one standard error on the mean, and the rule is kept because it is never worse; do not expect more from tuning it. It does not carry over to the other bots: giving `exileByAverage` to `mixed`, `build` and `smart` (seeds 1000–1099, greedy) lowered goal-48 hits from 47% to 43%, 43% to 38% and 23% to 21%, and goal-64 hits from 13% to 11%, 10% to 8% and 3% to 3%, so they keep the gold-Basic exile rule.
- `edgeFollowUp` redraws before defeating the edge enemy when its left neighbour is an enemy that the Power left after paying for the edge could not pay for, and the buying policies want nothing else in the row (only the edge take is vetoed). It is in `lab-human`, `mixed` and `build`, not `smart`, whose own redraw policy would veto the redraw and leave the edge enemy alive. Effect on seeds 1000–1099 × 400 greedy games, hits and mean ♥ before → after: `lab-human` 58% / 44.4 → 58% / 44.5 at goal 48 and 20% / 49.3 → 20% / 49.4 at goal 64; `mixed` 47% / 42.1 → 48% / 42.1 and 13% / 45.6 → 13% / 45.7; `build` 43% / 41.1 → 43% / 41.3 and 10% / 44.1 → 10% / 44.2. P10 and P90 did not move. It is never worse but the gain is at the noise level, because the case is rare; do not expect more from tuning it. Requiring two or more redraws in hand (so a bad redraw can be answered with another) changed nothing: the rule fires about 0.26 times per game, and 60–100% of those firings already had a spare redraw (all of them at goal 64), so the gate removes almost nothing; reverted. A first version that vetoed every take, recruits included, cost about 0.4 kills per game and gave no gain. The older `protectEdge` is inert for greedy bots: its redraw weight of 8 × the 0.2 idle redraw factor is far below an enemy take's weight.
- Bot baseline: 100 base seeds × 400 games per profile, using the same seeds before and after. The old greedy `mixed` profile averaged 36.2 ♥ at goal 48, 37.4 ♥ at goal 64, and 69.1 ♥ in Long Run; normalized sampled `mixed` averaged 30.2 ♥, 30.6 ♥, and 51.3 ♥. The new `random` control averaged −9.3 ♥, −9.3 ♥, and −41.1 ♥.
- At goal 48, `smart` moved from 19.1% to 9.5% hits; at goal 64 it moved from 2.4% to 0.5%. It stayed slightly ahead of `mixed` in Standard mean hearts after normalization, while Long Run `mixed` remained slightly ahead. All normal profiles stayed well above random.
- `lookahead` was measured separately because it is expensive: 100 games for each Standard goal and 20 Long Run games. The normalized version averaged 35.2 ♥ at goal 48, 37.7 ♥ at goal 64, and 78.5 ♥ in Long Run. The full metrics file `tools/baseline/bots-2026-11.json` was removed as outdated; it is in git history at commit `318b050`.
- Bot ideas that shipped in `lab-human`, so they are no longer open: phased buying (`phaseBuy`), the play order (`humanPlayOrder`), exile by deck average (`exileByAverage`) and `edgeFollowUp`, which is the "flush before taking the edge enemy" idea. Buying by deck-average uplift, alone, softly or as a veto, was tried and lost (first lines of this section), so do not retry it. The "one policy at a time" ladder is the `lab-random` to `lab-draw` line above.

## Undo and generation

- Undo-peek simulation: `npm run sim -- --analysis undo --games 1000 --seed 1 --mode standard --goal 1` sampled 23,000 turn states with no row purchases. The first preview card became the next row card 87.2% of the time; preview cards 2–4 matched the next-turn redraw cards 1–3 78.8% of the time; all four future positions matched 73.7% of the time. Generation thresholds made some turns much less predictable, especially T11, where all four matched 0.1% of the time.

## Generator and presets

- Weighted enemy-gain baseline: 2,000,000 Standard cards with seed 1 produced 8,895 recruit variants and 400 enemy variants; 700,915 enemies included 52,572 Gold cards and 52,415 Power cards, about 15% combined, with 64,512 Gold units, 64,291 Power units and 1,400,280 Hearts units. The alternate gain is scaled from enemy cost.
- Weighted enemy-gain bot comparison: 400 Standard goal-48 games with seed 1 changed `mixed` from 24.75% hits and 36.015 mean Hearts to 23.5% and 36.6825, `build` from 18.75% and 35.1325 to 22% and 36.22, `smart` from 8.5% and 29.5675 to 11.75% and 30.55, and `lab-human` from 35.25% and 40.1975 to 37.25% and 40.1475. Best runs changed from 54, 54, 53 and 60 Hearts to 57, 55, 54 and 55.
- Engine 0.0.8 against 0.0.9 with the same bot: the current `lab-human` policies run on the 0.0.8 engine (an export of `318b050` with `src/core/ai` copied over and two card-field reads adapted) and on 0.0.9, 100 seeds × 400 greedy games (1000–1099). Hits and mean ♥, 0.0.8 → 0.0.9: goal 48 55% / 43.7 → 58% / 44.5; goal 64 19% / 48.3 → 20% / 49.4; goal 80 4% / 49.4 → 4% / 50.6; goal 96 1% / 49.6 → 1% / 50.8. The 0.0.9 engine is not harder for this bot (about 1 ♥ easier), and its best goal-96 run went from 114 to 109 ♥. Part of the gap is the bot's own use of river exile, which 0.0.8 lacks.
- Generator variety: `node tools/cardcount.mjs --samples 100000 --seed 1` observed 4,340 recruit variants and estimated about 6,026; it observed 134 enemy variants and estimated about 140. The estimate includes rolled effect parameters and the current Standard generator.
- At the archived 2,000,000-sample size, the current Standard generator observed 8,888 recruit variants and 138 enemy variants; v6 observed 8,758 recruits and 126 enemies at the same sample size. The current generator has not lost variety.
- Constrained "simple only" run (first playtest): the pool felt very small (four identical 2 ● + 2 ⚔ Ember cards in a row), a few turns had nothing affordable, and with no exile or draw a few good cards carried the run. Level 1 was reached on turn 23. Bots (400 games, seed 1, goal 48): `mixed` hit 34% against 22% in Standard, `smart` 35% against 16%, so for bots the plain pool is easier. The bots' mean hearts at turn 24 was about 5 higher. At goals 64 and 80 neither mode was reachable for them (0 to 4%). It later became the Simple only preset.
- Resource switches came from a pacifist run (`types=moss,tide`) that wasted 48 ⚔ and ended on 43 of 48 ♥ with 0 defeated, because Basics still gave power. Plain gains, hearts and the Kin and Army currency are redirected to an allowed resource.
- Effect requirements (`EffectDef.needs`): `foes`, `weak` need enemies and power; `pile` needs power; `reapP` needs power and hearts; `coin`, `basics`, `cheap` need gold; `reapG` needs gold and hearts; `swap` needs gold and power; `all3` needs all three colours and hearts; `vault` needs exile and hearts.
- Presets: Standard, Simple only, Attack only, Pacifist. Pacifist is the playtested variant (Moss and Tide, gold and hearts, ten gold Basics). Attack only is `types=foe&res=power,hearts&sg=0&sp=5` with Foes and Weak. Its bot run gives 98% hits and about 92 hearts in 5 turns, because stacked Weak makes enemies free. Pacifist playtest: 80 ● wasted in a win on turn 22 with 54 ♥, exiled 10, recruited 19.
- Moss economy preset: a human won Level 3 with it (86 ♥ on turn 23), while the bots reach goal 48 only 9 to 12% of the time. Tide hearts (54 to 57%) and Ember combat (32 to 33%) are the other two. Bot numbers: 200 games, seed 1, goal 48.
- First bot numbers for goal 1 (200 games, seed 1, `mixed`): Standard 21%, Simple only 33%, Pacifist 22%, Attack only 98%.
- The original bot driver ended the turn by weighted chance, so an endless turn did not hang it; the sampled bot driver keeps the 80-move turn cap and 4000-move game cap as safety nets.
- Decided: the game is called Standard, not Classic. There is no separate Simple mode; the modes are Standard, Custom and Long Run, and `goal` (1 to 3) is apart from the mode. Old ids (`standard1` to `standard3`, `level2`, a bare `2`) still parse when there is no `goal`.
- Presets, the custom generator type and the step to a `ModeConfig` live in `src/core/custom.ts`; link reading and writing is in `src/platform/web/custom.ts`. Starting any new game rewrites the address, so a reload replays the same run. Pasting a link starts that run; the dialog (New game, Custom generator…) is for changing a generator.
- `drawFirst` plays a Pile card before a draw that would reshuffle. Standard bot numbers did not change (mixed 22%, smart 16%).
- Bots end the turn by weighted chance, so an endless turn does not hang them; the 80-move turn cap and the 4000-move game cap are safety nets.
- Old title-screen concepts and the home screen sketch were not reused; the code started again for the custom generator.

## Bots

- Ember combat preset: the first version had no gold effects, and its recruits (which pay out in power) could not be bought, so the bots bought nothing (32% at goal 48). Adding Exile, Coin and Swap gave 54 to 58%, and a 7 ● / 3 ⚔ start (against 5/5, 3/7 and 8/2) gave 60 to 67%; 3/7 was worse than 5/5 (20 to 32%) and 8/2 was easier (68 to 72%). Seeds 2 and 3, 400 games: 56 to 70% at goal 48 and 19 to 25% at goal 64. A human run on the earlier version wasted 44 ● and 29 ⚔ in a 54 of 80 ♥ finish.
- `smart` against `mixed` (ablation, 400 games, goal 48, seeds 1 and 2, throwaway script): `smartRedraw` is the strong part and `smartBuy` the weak part. `mixed` with `smartRedraw` hits 73 to 74% on Ember combat (against 62 to 64% for `mixed`) and 28% in Standard (against 22%). `mixed` with `smartBuy` is worse than `mixed` in both (52 to 53% and 14%). `smart` itself beats `mixed` on Ember combat (65 to 70%) but loses in Standard (16%), because the redraw gain is the larger effect on Ember and the buying loss is the larger one in Standard. `smartBuy` prefers cheap cards; `mixed`'s `buyValue` prefers dear ones.
- Human redraw ablation (Standard goal 48, greedy selection, 100 seeds × 400 games): the current average-cost rule averaged 42.4 ♥ with 47% goal hits; adding `protectEdge` was unchanged; adding `smartRedraw` alongside the current rules fell to 40.7 ♥ and 38%; replacing the current redraw rules with `smartRedraw` fell to 39.2 ♥ and 31%. `smartRedraw` vetoes redraws when its own condition is false, so it suppresses the average-cost rule.
- Cycle/Purge direct-gain experiment (Standard goal 48, greedy selection, 100 seeds × 400 games): including the Cycle/Purge card's own direct resources in the readiness comparison improved `lab-human` from 42.4 ♥ and 47% goal hits to 42.9 ♥ and 50%. The goal-64 worst replay after the change was −9 ♥ on seed 5000017.
- `phaseBuy` effect values (100 seeds × 400 greedy games, 1000–1099, `lab-human`, hits and mean ♥ at goals 48 / 64 / 96): counting Cycle and Purge as one family when halving repeated copies moved 58% / 44.5, 20% / 49.4, 1% / 50.8 to 58% / 44.6, 21% / 49.6, 1% / 51.0. Exile at 5.5 instead of 5 gave 58% / 44.6, 21% / 49.6, 1% / 51.1, and at 7 gave 58% / 44.6, 21% / 49.7, 1% / 51.2 (P90 72 at goal 96); exiles per game rose from 5.6 to 5.8. All changes are at noise level and none was worse, so family counting and Exile at 7 are kept. A higher Exile value was not tried.
- Redraw reserve allowance (Standard goal 48, greedy selection, 100 seeds × 400 games): lowering the average-cost threshold by `(redraws − 1) / 5` when more than one redraw remains moved `lab-human` from 42.9 ♥ to 43.0 ♥ on average, with the same 50% goal-hit rate.

## Animation and interface

- Cycle and Purge draw before discarding their selected hand cards; the core rule arrived in `8d41167`, and `9168b1d` added the discard-flight presentation. A later full-reload check found the order and animation clear.
- SVG resource, Retain and lock icons plus scaled cards arrived in `c1be2b3`; `9168b1d` added the related animation settings. A full-reload check found no remaining 1 px icon shift and the Extra slow setting present.
- Resources fly to their stat boxes, and the boxes pulse on arrival. Flights are purely visual and never hold the view back. A flight shows the move's total, lifts, drifts, then speeds to its box. Every change to a box pulses it once. Exiled cards fly to the piles box like recruits. The "Extra slow" speed step is for inspecting.
- A stat box keeps its old number while a flight carries the change (`hold` and `release` in the store). Spending pulses on the click. Flights from one card fan out. A redraw shrinks the four old cards in a stagger and the rightmost goes first. Cards that move because the hand or row changed slide from their old place (`flip`).
- Cards that leave the hand are found by comparing the old and new state (`departingIds`), so exile, cycle, purge and end of turn all fly them to the piles. A card counts as kept only if it was not drawn in the same move. This fixed end-turn cards that stayed still and then vanished with a small deck. A test covers it.
- The presenter plans the next frame only after the view has drawn the previous one. A cap on the total stagger was tried and removed; the last cards leaving together looked worse than a longer wave.
- Input is queued while the logic stays immediate. A click resolves the card by its id, so a card that shifts during an animation is never clicked by mistake. Undo, new game and reduced motion skip the queue. Resource flights ignore the rush.
- Presentation lag: `GameStore` keeps the latest session and a shown session, and each move is a frame with `before` and `after` sessions.
- The turn and discount boxes pulse; a held number counts up one flight at a time. Drawing pulses the piles box. The exile prompt has an idle striped animation that stops under reduced motion.
- Piles overview colours: gold figures yellow, power orange, hearts pink, and each type count in its type colour. Four lines: deck size, plain gains, effects, types. Below 450 px the columns drop and rows flow inline.
- The hovered or focused card gets a glow in its own colour. Cards are drawn at a fixed 118 × 175 and scaled with a CSS `scale`, so small cards do not inflate under a browser minimum font size.
- Icons are web only (`src/platform/web/icons/`). The core keeps the unicode symbols as text, and `Rich` wraps each symbol in a span with a transparent text colour and the icon as background, so copied text stays unicode.
- "Swap ● and ⚔" is the Swap text because the older text was 117.98 px of a 118 px card.
- Browser trick for inspection: `document.getAnimations().forEach((a) => a.pause())` freezes every running animation (resume with `play()`).
- Reward for spending exactly all gold and power in a turn (a bonus heart or a free draw) felt good in two playtests in a row.
- Card art drift: a first version moved each shape a few pixels over 18 to 40 seconds, and the designer could not tell whether it moved; three times faster (6 to 13 seconds) still looked like the fast setting of what the designer imagined, so it is now 3 to 6.5 seconds at the normal setting. A hover push was added. The animation setting scales both like every other animation.
- The right-edge ring used to be a class on the card, so it left with the card and then jumped to the next card as it slid into the fifth slot (seen once Shift and Push could move the edge card); it now belongs to the river's fifth slot. The hover push of the art also took four tries: a `:hover` rule and CSS-only latches (a delayed transition with a style query, and a discrete animation-name latch) could not play to the end after the pointer left and also restart from the current place, so the push is a Web Animation.
- A new game deals its cards (the river from the right like a flush, then the hand like a draw) from `planDeal`, triggered by the store's `getDeals` counter. An undo also changes the store's epoch, so the epoch alone cannot tell a fresh deal from an undo.
- New game links say `screen=play` instead of `mode=custom` (`hasRun` still reads any `mode`, so old links work). The `mode` value was never read before, only its presence; it now carries the preset name (`mode=simple`), so a link from an older version keeps its preset name with an asterisk when the preset changes later.
- Disabled cards used opacity 0.55, which showed the ring and the art behind them; they now share the played-card filter (`brightness(0.72) saturate(0.5)`), so a disabled river card and a played hand card look alike.
- An 11-card hand on a phone needed careful aiming, so from 10 cards the hand staggers into two rows (`.hand.large`, every other card half a card lower); it stays playable to about 17 cards. The fixed-edge marker is the ring on the river's fifth slot with a lock, which replaced the lock on the card.
- Copy run on a phone over an insecure LAN address first showed "Not copied", since the Clipboard API needs a secure origin. Every Copy button now falls back to a selected read-only text box under it, so the text can be copied by hand.
- The row heading said "Recruit row" while cards, logs and the Home text said "river", and the row holds enemies as well; the heading became "River" when Shift (the first effect named after the river) shipped.

## Results and statistics

- Challenge results keep two separate records per goal, the best hearts and the earliest winning turn, so a faster win with fewer hearts does not replace a slower one with more. Each entry also stores the game version of the run that last changed it. Entries saved before that were never given a migration: the only ones were the designer's, backfilled by hand. Wins are never dropped on a version change. The designer's first results (Moss economy, Attack only, Pacifist, Tide hearts, Ember combat, Simple only at Level 1) were backfilled by hand with the version `0.1.0` and the turns he had logged.
- Results move between devices and origins through Settings: Copy results, and Paste results with a merge that keeps the better record of each pair. The localStorage of the dev server and of GitHub Pages are separate origins, and a dev port that changes starts a clean slate, so there was no other way. A pasted run replay is refused with a pointer to Paste replay, so a mis-paste cannot replace a game.
- The run summary names what a run improved in its goal's record ("New best: 60 ♥ (was 57)", "First win", "Fastest win: turn 19 (was 22)"). It came from a Moss economy Level 2 try that went from 57 to 60 ♥ without winning, with nothing to show for it. The lines are kept in storage with the address of the run, so a reload still shows them for that run and for no other.
- Hearts by source (`stats.heartsFrom`) and the run summary dialog came from two playtests (Moss economy and Pacifist) that both ended with the question where the hearts came from. The sources are `plain`, `defeat` and each effect by id; they are measured as the change in hearts around each effect, so no effect file knows about it. Gold and power by source are not done.
- Bond and Flip lost their name prefix on cards ("Bond: +2 ⚔ …"). One prefix looked odd and prefixes on every effect risk overflowing the card, so only Retain keeps its name, because other effect text refers to it.

## Effect origins

- Bulk (an enemy that needs less ⚔ for every few cards in your deck) came from a playtest where a 10-card deck won with 89 ♥ on turn 22: thin decks were too strong against enemies. Bulk and Hoard (`+a ●` per few cards in the deck) shipped in 0.0.10, and the numbers are in the Generator and presets section. Neither is in a preset yet.

- Echo (0.2.0) repeats the effects of the card played just before it, except Echo, and not that card's gains. It replaced two older ideas: an Untap effect (replay a selected played card, so Ember could reuse scarce gold without Swap, with the replay suppressing the selected card's own Untap so it cannot recurse) and a duplicate-the-rightmost-unused-card effect. The difference is a free play of a chosen card against a repeat of the last one; Echo was chosen as the clearer sentence. Reload, Push and Shift shipped with it: Push (move the right-most enemy to the left end) because Attack only needed a third effect, Shift (new river cards on the left, the right edge falls off), and Reload (draw per enemy defeated this turn, up to 3).
- Play on after a win (0.2.0, the move `continue`) answers "stop early or carry on" and "carry on after the goal": the player may stop at the goal turn or play to the last turn, and the win stays. A mode where only a separate "Claim win" move wins, scored by final hearts, was considered and not built.
- Echo played well in the 0.2.0 playtests (with plain gains, with Swap and with enemies), so the feared double reward from a big enemy did not appear and the "repeat only recruits" fix was not needed. An Attack only playtest in the same version reached turns above the usual range and found the long-turn scaling not too punishing.
- Echo was widened before it shipped (0.2.0, after a playtest where the effects-only rule felt weak and fizzled on basic cards): it now repeats the last card played or enemy defeated in full, gains included, and costs 3 instead of 2. The designer expected a second Echo to repeat the first Echo's card without its effect, which the rule now does.

## Research terms

- `Gauntlet` in Gemblades unlocks after Invasion V and combines the five invasions into a harder mode with starting-deck customisation and its own leaderboard.
- `Hoard` is not identified as a Gemblades mode in the store or update material I checked. Unresolved until the original review is found.
- `Familiars` are summoned companions in Gemblades, a separate layer from recruited cards: unique, capped at twelve, the oldest is removed on a thirteenth, and they act immediately.
