# Heartrow design

## Purpose

Heartrow is a small, single-player roguelike deckbuilder. A run lasts a fixed number of turns. The player builds a deck of procedurally generated recruits, defeats enemies, and collects hearts.

The working instructions are in [`AGENTS.md`](../AGENTS.md). This document holds the durable design decisions. Ideas that are not done are in [`ideas.md`](ideas.md). Finished work and playtest evidence are in [`history.md`](history.md).

## Principles

- Core and platform stay apart. `src/core` is the game: plain TypeScript with no platform dependencies. `applyMove(state, move)` returns a new state and a list of events, and all randomness comes from seeded streams stored in the state. A platform only draws the state and sends moves.
- Modes, presets and generator limits are data, not code. A difference in numbers or names is a field. A difference in behaviour is a function-valued field on a small record. No inheritance and no `if (variant === 'x')` in shared code.
- Seed stability. A seed makes the same run. Generator limits only redirect results and never change the random draws, so a generator that allows everything makes the same cards as no generator. Undo replays random outcomes identically.
- Events carry presentation. `applyMove` returns `{ state, events }` with plain-data events. The web animation layer plays them as a queue, so the state is already final and animation is only presentation that can be skipped. The game log and statistics read the same events. Bots and the simulator ignore them.
- Core hands out neutral data (type, cost, effect text). Colours, glyphs, layout and settings (theme, animation speed) belong to the platform and never to the game state.
- Reasoning lives here, not in code comments. Do not implement an open question as if it were approved.
- Prefer small changes that improve decisions or remove confusion.
- One simple loop, many ways to play it. Content comes from varied styles of play (modes, generator presets, self-imposed restrictions that are measured) and from guiding players to try them, not from a single high score to beat. Custom generators are the modding story.

## Where things live

- `src/core` is the game. It owns the rules, the card generator and its limits, modes, presets, the effect registry and the bots. Other platforms use only `src/core/index.ts`.
- `src/platform/web` is the Preact front end. It owns links and the address bar, dialogs, animation, skins and styling. Reading and writing links (`mode.ts`, `custom.ts`) is web only; the data a link describes is core.
- `tools/` holds the simulator. It uses core only.
- `prototypes/` holds the original single-file prototypes. They are frozen references and history, not the specification. When the prototypes and the code disagree, the code and this document win.

## Player experience

The game should be playable without a tutorial. The player should understand the row, the two currencies, the edge penalty, and the current goal from the screen and the card text.

The central decision is positional:

- Five cards move from left to right.
- A new card enters on the left.
- Taking a card removes it and shifts the cards on its left toward the edge.
- The card on the right leaves at the end of the turn.
- An enemy that leaves the row removes its hearts from the player's score.

This makes buying a card beside an enemy a meaningful choice. Buying the edge card also moves its left neighbour to the edge.

## Rules

- Gold buys recruits. Power defeats enemies. Hearts are the score.
- Gold and power are turn-local. They do not carry over.
- Recruits enter the discard pile. Enemies never enter the deck.
- The starter deck has seven Basic gold cards and three Basic power cards. With the 20% enemy share of the first turns, a power-heavier start left little to spend power on early (hand runs) and cost the bots 5 to 7 points of hit rate at goal 48.
- The hand normally draws five cards.
- Retain cards stay in hand when unplayed.
- Flush replaces the four left cards and leaves the edge card in place.
- Reaching the goal wins only when the turn ends.
- “Play simple” plays plain gains and cards whose effects all need no decision (the `unconditional` flag on an effect: Draw, Flip, Cheap, Weak, ReapG, ReapP, Flush, Hoard and Army; Hoard and Army count the deck, and one purchase in a turn rarely reaches the next step; what the others pay does not depend on the order of play or the board), except Retain cards, which stay in the hand. When no such card is left the same button reads “Play rest” and plays every card still in the hand, in order, including cards with effects and Retain cards. The button keeps the width of the longer label, centred, so the button row does not shift. The space key is the shortcut and is named in the tooltip only.
- Exile is optional, and an Exile 2 card asks twice. The player can skip it. It can target any hand card, or a recruit in river slots 1–4; enemies and the right-edge card are not valid targets. Exiling a river recruit refills the left edge.
- Undo is permissive: any move except ending the turn can be undone, back to the start of the turn. Random outcomes replay identically, so undo cannot fish for luck.

Cards are generated when they enter the row. Their type, resources, effects, and art come from the generator budget. There is no row deck.

The exact order of events in a turn is defined by `endTurn` in `src/core/moves.ts` and its tests.

### Modes

Modes are data (`ModeConfig` in `src/core/modes.ts`), not code.

- **Standard** lasts 24 turns at the goals 1 to 4: 48, 64, 80 or 96 hearts. It is the default generator (every type, resource and effect) like any other: `standardRun(goal)` in `src/core/custom.ts`. The goal is apart from the generator (`goal=` in the link).
- **Custom** is Standard with a generator: which card types, resources and effects may appear, and how many Basic gold and power cards you start with. The settings are plain data (`CustomGenerator` in `src/core/custom.ts`) with a small library of presets (Standard, Simple only, Attack only, Pacifist, Moss economy, Tide hearts, Ember combat). The web layer puts them in the link, so a run can be shared (`?screen=play&seed=N&mode=simple&goal=2&types=…&res=…&sg=…&sp=…&fx=…`).
- **Goals** are plain records in `GOALS` (`src/core/modes.ts`): a key (`1` to `4`, `long`), a target and the deadline in turns. All goals share one ramp (`DIFFICULTY_STEPS`), so a new goal is a new record with no scaling of its own. Every challenge offers every goal, and a link names one with `goal=`. A run is won, and ends, when a turn ends with the target met; `goalMetTurn` keeps that turn.
- **Playing on.** Every goal works the same after a win: the run summary (which the log dialog reopens) and the action bar offer "Keep playing to turn N", which is the move `continue`. N is `playOnTurns` (42), also for a level won on its last turn at 24. A win before the goal's last turn first offers "Keep playing to turn 24" (that score still counts), and the run summary opens again at turn 24 with the offer to go on to turn 42, where the summary and the button's tooltip say that hearts after turn 24 are not recorded (`continueTarget` in `moves.ts`). The hearts beyond the goal are a score, and the win still counts after later losses (the result keeps the most hearts and the earliest turn the target was met). A run that misses its target by the deadline is lost, and it may still play on, as a score only: the goal counts only when met by the deadline (`withinDeadline` in `moves.ts`), so a late goal is neither a win nor a record, and the run ends as lost. The best hearts of a goal are the hearts at the end of the goal's last turn (24), or at the turn of the win when the run stops there (`heartsAtDeadline` in `challenges.ts`); hearts scored on turns 25 to 42 are for information and never enter any record, and goals are not mixed. Bots never choose it; the simulator does it for Long Run (`playOn`), and for any goal with `--play-on`, so those numbers are hearts at the last turn.
- **The ramp** is the same for every goal (`DIFFICULTY_STEPS` in `src/core/modes.ts`). The share of new cards that are enemies is 20% in turns 1 to 6 and 35% from turn 7: in the first turns a crowded river leaves little to recruit and the deck cannot clear it, so a flood belongs after the deck has started to grow. A first try of 15%, 25% and 35% (from turns 1, 9 and 17) cost the bots about 22 points of hit rate at goal 48 (see `docs/history.md`). Turns 25–36 add one cost step and use 40% enemy frequency; turns 37–42 add a second cost step and use 45%. Late pure-resource filler is bounded and rerolled more often from turn 21 (`lateFillerFromTurn`), while utility cards stay eligible. The run grants one flush every three turns; a four-turn cadence left too little row control late in the run.
- **Long Run** is the goal `long`: 200 ♥ in 42 turns, the same ramp with a deadline of 42. The target is a first guess from bot data, to be balanced.

Every run is a generator and every link spells it out, Standard included: `?screen=play&seed=N&mode=standard&goal=2&types=moss,ember,tide,foe&res=gold,power,hearts&sg=6&sp=4&fx=draw,flip,…`. A link that lists its effects keeps its game valid when effects are added later, because the effects it does not name are off. `screen=play` starts the run (a seed alone does not; any `mode=…` also starts one, so old links work); a part a link leaves out means everything, and an address with no goal is the first goal. The link also names the preset the run was started as (`mode=simple`; `custom` names none), and a run is that preset by name, so a link from an older version keeps its name when the preset's settings change later. The link ends with the game version it was made in (`v=0.2.0`). A run whose link was made in another version, or whose settings no longer equal the named preset, shows the preset name with an asterisk, and the run dialog explains why. With other settings it counts towards no goal; with equal settings from another version it still counts, and the note only says it may not play as it did. A link without a name (`mode=custom`, or an old link) is the preset its settings equal, or Custom. Starting any new game rewrites the address, so a reload replays the same run.

### Screens

There are five screens, home, game, Standard, the challenge picker and the generator editor, and the address says which. An address with no `mode` or `seed` is the home screen; one with either starts that run directly; `?screen=custom` plus the generator settings is the editor, `?screen=standard` is the Standard picker (the four levels and Long Run as big buttons, each showing its best result), and `?screen=challenges` is the challenge picker, and none of them starts a run by itself. The challenge picker's address also names the card and goal (`&challenge=moss&goal=3`, or `goal=long`), kept up to date as the player moves, so a reload or a link opens the same place. The home screen lists Resume (only when a run is in memory), Standard, Challenges, Settings and About. The challenge picker is one screen high: a scroll-snapping row of cards (Standard, the generator presets of `src/core/custom.ts`, and Custom last), round icon dots that jump to a card, the goals of the current card in a list that scrolls (Level 1 to 4, and Long Run for Standard only), and Start pinned at the bottom. A card shows one star per goal. A finished run is credited to a challenge goal by its mode, or for a custom run by its generator matching a preset at any goal, and the best hearts and the win are kept in `localStorage` (`results`). Replays do not count, Custom keeps no results, and Long Run keeps the best hearts without a win. Every screen change adds a history entry, so Back returns to the screen you came from. An address that matches the run in memory resumes it, and any other game address starts that run.

The editor keeps its settings in the address as you change them, so a reload or a copied address gives the same editor. It opens from the home screen (with the settings of the last run, if there is one) and from the run dialog's "Open in the generator editor" button (with the settings of the run on screen; a Standard run opens with the defaults at its goal). Back from the editor returns to where you came from, for example the run; Start begins a new custom run and replaces the one in memory. The screen state is web only; core knows nothing about screens.

The game screen has only game actions in its button bar (Keep playing after a win, End turn, Play simple or Play rest, Flush, Undo). Once a run is over, End turn, Play simple, Flush and Undo have nothing to do, so a "Run summary" button takes their place. The log dialog (the status line opens it) has the same button full width on top, then the log; copying the seed, link and run is in the run dialog only. It sits between the river and the hand, so the river with its title and the hand with its title each read as one unit. Skip exile sits next to the exile prompt in the status line below the hand, so the bar never changes size. While a bot replay is loaded the bar shows only Next move, Undo and Take over (which ends the replay and keeps the position). The hand title shows the card count ("Your hand · 17") and the gains the hand adds, with two view-only tools: a sort button (D draw order, T type, E effect; each click moves to the next) and an eye button that hides cards already played. Neither changes the game. Home sits at the top left; New game, Settings and About are on the home screen. The header's other button shows the mode in its own look (see "Look of modes", for example Moss economy for a preset run, otherwise Standard or Custom) with an info icon, and opens a dialog with the level, goal, turns, the generator summary of a custom run, and the seed with its copy buttons.

### Saved run

The web layer saves the current run in `localStorage` after every accepted move, undo and new game. The save is the run's address (mode, goal, seed, generator settings), the accepted moves and the app version. The core is pure and its random streams live in the state, so replaying the moves rebuilds the exact run, including the undo stack of the current turn. A reload (for example when the browser drops a background tab) resumes it: an address that names the saved run replays it however far it got, and a plain home address offers Resume. A finished run stays resumable until a new game replaces it. Any other game address starts that run and replaces the save. A save from another version, or one the rules refuse to replay, is dropped. For that reason the version in `package.json` is bumped whenever engine behaviour changes. The run dialog's "Copy run" button (the mode button in the header opens it, and it also holds Copy seed and Copy link) copies the same record (every Copy button falls back to a selected read-only text box under it when the browser refuses the clipboard, as a phone on a plain HTTP address does), so a run can be replayed for a bug report (`npm run sim -- --replay <file>`). A saved run is kept as a reference (`tests/fixtures/reference-run.json`, a `smart` bot game written by `npm run sim -- --worst`); a test replays it and checks the end state, so a change to engine behaviour cannot slip through unnoticed.

## Card generation

- Recruit cost is a converted budget, not a direct value sum: a recruit with base cost `C` starts with `C × .85 + base` budget, where the base is 0.1 for Moss, 0.3 for Ember and 0.5 for Tide (`RECRUIT_BUDGET_BASE`). A cost steps by less than one budget unit, so whole-number spends skip one cost; each type skips a different one, so the mix has no notch. A preset with one type keeps its notch. The row may display a lower current price when a recruit discount is active.
- Direct gold and power each cost `1`, hearts cost `1.5`, and effects consume their listed budget. Integer resource gains can leave a fractional remainder below `1`; this is intentional. For example, a base-cost-12 card with `+2 ● +4 ⚔ +3 ♥` uses `10.5` budget exactly, while a base-cost-11 card with `+8 ● +1 ⚔` leaves `0.65`.
- Enemy budget is the cost times `1.4`. The displayed cost is recomputed from the budget actually spent.
- Direct gains are one unit at a time. Recruits keep their existing lean and resource distribution. Enemies have a 15% chance to spend part of their budget on Gold or Power instead of Hearts; the alternate gain is `max(1, floor(cost × .4))`, and its budget is reserved before effects and Hearts are generated.
- Draw is one scalable effect that rolls 1 to 4 cards and is priced by tier (see "Price systems"), so a bigger draw needs a much more expensive recruit.
- Any recruit effect can roll Retain. Each effect sets its own `retain` terms (budget price and chance), taken from three tiers in `generator/budget.ts`. The price is always `0.5`. The chance follows how often waiting helps: 70% for `push` (it only helps when the right-most enemy is the one to hide); 50% for `exile`, `cycle`, `cheap`, `weak`, `foes`, `all3`, `pile`, `reapG`, `reapP` and `shift` (worth it only when the edge card is junk); 30% for `swap`, `bond`, `kin`, `basics`, `flip`, `redraw`, `reload` and `echo`; 10% for `army`, `hoard`, `vault` and `draw`. About a third of effect recruits carry Retain, and 10% of plain recruits (`PLAIN_RETAIN_CHANCE`, the same price of `0.5`, so a plain +2 with Retain fits cost 3). The generator's `keywords` list (`kw=` in the link; a missing `kw` means all, an empty one means none) switches Retain off everywhere. The Retain draws are taken even when it is off, so the limits only redirect the result. Retain is free only for the next draw: kept cards still take room among the cards you draw, and Redraw discards them. Enemies never roll Retain.
- Scaled conditional effects should use simple magnitude pricing of roughly `3` budget per level; test the exact values for discounts and per-enemy effects before resolving them.
- Direct `♥` resources are intentionally less common on Moss and Ember recruits than on Tide recruits; dedicated heart effects remain the stronger heart-building identity. Once a recruit gets a direct heart, generation favours another heart so multi-heart cards remain meaningful.
- The two discard effects are `cycle`, which keeps Retain cards, and `redraw`, which discards Retain cards too. Both cost 1.5 and lean Tide. When the deck and discard pile hold fewer cards than the draw needs, the effect draws what they hold, discards the unplayed hand cards (Retain cards stay for Cycle), and draws the shortfall from a reshuffle that includes the cards it just discarded.
- `all3` rolls its resource (● ⚔ ♥, among the allowed ones) and its amount (2 to 4 ♥, or 3 to 5 ● or ⚔). It costs two thirds of its payout, so 2 ♥ costs 2. The amounts start where the price reaches the recruit cost floor of 2: below it an All3 would cost the same as a plain card with the same gain and no condition. A card without rolled values reads +2 ♥.
- `swap` exchanges gold and power. It leans Moss and Ember and gives a gold engine an outlet for defeating enemies.
- **Leans.** A recruit has a currency lean (Moss gold, Ember power, Tide half and half) and an effect lean: the effects listing its type in `lean` share 70% of the pool weight (`LEAN_SHARE`) and the rest share 30%. An effect that lists two types gets the lean weight on both; one that lists all three is simply more common everywhere. Tide recruits also roll an effect more often (75% against 45%). Enemies have no effect lean, so `bulk` lists none. `mildLean` adds one extra copy, which is about twice an unlean effect and far less than a lean. Moss: `flip`, `bond`, `basics`, `reapG`, `kin`, `swap`, `hoard`, `army`, `push`, `exile`. Ember: `weak`, `foes`, `reload`, `reapP`, `swap`, `exile`, `vault`, `shift`, `flush`. Tide: `draw`, `cycle`, `redraw`, `cheap`, `all3`, `hoard`, `army`, `pile`, `kin`, `exile`. Every type leans on `exile`, and `mildLean` is empty for now. No lean (30% share only): `echo`. With every effect in the pool a lean effect is about 4 to 6 times as likely as an unlean one on its type. Ember buys the fewest cards, so Pile (a discard pile count) leans Tide, and Kin leans Moss and Tide. Push, a river effect, leans Moss: Ember already has Shift and Flush and is not meant to be the river type.
- **Payout currency.** Every effect that pays a resource (`flip`, `bond`, `kin`, `army`, `all3`, `hoard`, `basics`, `pile`) rolls it with `rollPayoutCurrency` in `effects/payout.ts`, which uses the lean of a plain gain: hearts first (7.5%, 16.5% on Tide), then the card's other resource (15%), otherwise its main one. A resource that the generator switches off, or that the budget cannot pay for, gives its chance to the others in proportion to their own (`pickPayoutResource`, used by plain gains too), so the draw count stays fixed. The price is the gold price times `RESOURCE_PRICE`, so hearts cost 1.5 times as much: a Flip of tier 1 to 3 costs 2.3, 4.5 and 6.8 in hearts (`scaleTerms` in `effects/tiers.ts`). `vault`, `reapG` and `reapP` always pay hearts.
- Rolled output parameters live in `Card.fxParams`. Kin can produce Hearts on Tide when Hearts are enabled; Basics, Pile and Coin use the card's generated output currency. Parameterized output remains visible in card text and is priced before the remaining budget is spent.
- **Price systems.** Every effect is priced by one of four systems, and a new effect should pick one. (1) A flat price: most utility effects cost 1.5 (`swap`, `army`, `kin`, `cycle`, `redraw`, `reapG`, `reapP`, `flush`), and conditional ones less than their payoff would suggest (`reload` 2, `push` 1.25, `echo` 3). (2) Per unit: `shift` costs 0.75 per card. (3) Tiers with an offset (`TIER_TERMS` in `generator/budget.ts`, `effects/tiers.ts`): a tier costs `unit × tier − offset`. `weak`, `cheap` and `exile` use unit 3 and offset 1.5 (1.5 for tier 1, 4.5 for tier 2), `foes` uses unit 2.5 and offset 0.5 (2 and 4.5, for 1 or 2 ⚔ per enemy), `draw` uses unit 2.5 and offset 1 for 1 to 4 cards (1.5, 4, 6.5 and 9; the same terms as Weak, Cheap and Exile would give 7.5 and 10.5, which almost never fit a recruit's budget). Of the Draw cards a Standard run can roll, 68% are Draw 1, 27% Draw 2, 4.7% Draw 3 and 0.4% Draw 4; in a Long run's 42 turns 55%, 30%, 12% and 3.4%. At 1.5 per card Standard gave 46%, 31%, 16% and 5.6%. The Draw price came from a Tide run that reached 2,194 ♥ with hands of 20 to 25 cards, `bond` uses 0.8 and 0.2 for 1 to 5 (0.6, 1.4, 2.2, 3.0 and 3.8; a tier of 3 hearts costs 3.3, a plain +3 ♥ 4.5, so the condition is discounted by about a quarter), and `flip` uses 1.5 and 0 (1.5, 3, 4.5). (4) Count gain (`COUNT_GAIN_TERMS`): `0.75 × amount × unit × reference / cards per step`, for `hoard`, `basics`, `pile` and `vault`, with `all3` at two thirds of its payout. The half-integer prices of systems 1 and 3 are what fill cost 3 and cost 6 of the recruit cost curve, because a recruit's cost steps by 0.85 budget while plain gains step by 1.
- `exile` rolls an amount of 1 or 2 (tier terms above) and reads "Exile a card or river recruit" or "Exile 2 cards or river recruits". `pendingExile` counts the exiles still owed; each exile takes one, skipping ends them all, and the count drops to 0 once no hand card or river recruit is left to exile. `flip` rolls a tier `t` and pays `t` or `2t + 1` (1 or 3, 2 or 5, 3 or 7), shown as "+2 or +5 ●"; a card without a rolled tier counts as tier 2.
- **Recruit cost curve.** The share of generated recruits by cost, measured with `node tools/cardcount.mjs --samples 400000 --seed 1 --preset <id> --costs`. Standard: 2 11.1%, 3 15.8%, 4 20.0%, 5 18.5%, 6 14.1%, 7 11.0%, 8 6.2%, 9 2.4%, 10 0.7%, 11 0.2%, a hump with no notch (with one base for all types it had 3 at 8.0% and 6 at 9.7%, and Simple only 4.1% at cost 3). A preset with one type keeps its notch: Tide has 25.6% at cost 3 and 11.3% at cost 4, Ember 7.2% at cost 3. Enemies run 1 to 13 with no gap: 1 20%, 2 22%, 3 18%, 4 14%, 5 14%, 6 7%.
- `weak` and `cheap` roll an amount of 1 or 2 (shared `effects/tiers.ts`). Price is `3 × amount − 1.5`: 1.5 for −1 and 4.5 for −2, so the second rung needs much more budget. `weak` reads "Enemies need −N ⚔ this turn", `cheap` "Recruits cost −N ● this turn", and `bulk` also says "Needs".
- `hoard`, `basics`, `pile` and `vault` pay `+N` per D cards of one pile, with N 1 to 2 and D from `COUNT_GAIN_TERMS` in `generator/budget.ts` (Hoard counts the deck, Basics the Basic cards you own, Pile the discard, Vault the exile; Vault pays hearts, the others the card's main currency). The price is `0.75 × N × unit price × R / D`, rounded to a tenth: R is the reference count of the pile (16 for the deck, 8 for Basics, 6 for the discard, 5.3 for exile) and the factor allows for the smaller piles of early turns. They share `countGain.ts`, and all can roll on enemies. D is 4 to 6 for Hoard and 3 to 5 for the others, so the old fixed text (`1 per 3` for Vault and Pile, `1 per 4` for Basics) is one variant of each.
- `bulk` is an enemy-only passive effect: the enemy costs 1 ⚔ less per 4 cards in your deck, down to 0. The generator rolls the enemy's normal cost and budget, then prints the cost of a 16-card deck on top, so a thinner deck pays more and a fatter one pays less. Its price is 0; the reward is that of the normal cost. It exists to punish thin decks, which every logged Level 4 win used.
- `foes` counts the enemies in the row before the defeated enemy is replaced, including the enemy being defeated and excluding the newcomer.
- **River cards of a long turn.** Every card that enters the river during play (the replacement for a purchase, a flush, the exile of a river card, a Shift and the card at the end of the turn) counts in `generatedThisTurn`. The next card is made with the difficulty of the turn plus one turn for every 7 made so far this turn (`CARDS_PER_EXTRA_TURN`, `makeRiverCard`). The count starts again every turn, so an ordinary turn is unchanged and a long chain of purchases meets harder cards, which stops endless chains such as Attack only's without limiting how many moves a turn can have. Five flushes show cards of about three turns later.
- `flush` (recruits only) gives one flush when played, in addition to the one every `flushEvery` turns. It costs 1.5 (`FLUSH_PRICE`), Echo repeats it, and no preset lists it yet.
- `reload` draws 1 card per enemy defeated this turn (`defeatedThisTurn`, the enemy that carries it included), up to 3. `push` moves the right-most enemy of the river to the left end, wherever it is, and does nothing when it is already there. `shift` makes 1 to 3 new river cards on the left and lets as many cards fall off the right edge; an enemy that falls off costs its hearts like an escape. `echo` repeats the last card you played or enemy you defeated this turn (`lastUsed`, a copy): its gold, power and hearts and its effects, with Bond counting the card as played; it repeats Exile too, which asks again. A played Echo becomes the last card, so a second Echo repeats the first one's gains and other effects but not its Echo, and an Echo with nothing before it does nothing. It costs 3 (`ECHO_PRICE`) because an echoed enemy gives its hearts a second time.
- **Effect families and text.** Each effect has one `family` (cards, resources, counting cards, same type, prices, end of turn, river, repeat), kept together in the generator list in the order of `EFFECT_FAMILIES`. The families show where effects overlap (Basics and Army both count cards of a set; ReapG and ReapP are one reap; Hoard, Pile and Vault count three piles). The effects keep their ids, because links and saved runs name them. An effect writes its sentence once, in `describe(card?)`: with a card it is that card's text, and without one it is the sentence for the generator list with every value the effect can roll in brackets (`[1-2] [●/⚔]`), built from the same slots (`wording.ts`).

### Generator limits

`GeneratorConfig` (on `ModeConfig`) lists the allowed card types (Moss, Ember, Tide, enemies), resources (gold, power, hearts) and effects. `generatorLimits` in `src/core/generator/limits.ts` turns it into what can really be made.

- Plain gains, hearts and the Kin and Army currency are redirected to an allowed resource. Gold can be switched off too; the core falls back to gold only when no currency is allowed. Basics, Pile and Coin use an allowed output currency, and Kin may use Hearts for Tide.
- Enemies need power (to defeat) and hearts (to pay out), so they are off when either is.
- `EffectDef.needs` and `needsAny` list what an effect requires (resources, enemies, colours, the exile effect). The generator skips an effect whose need is off. A tripwire test lists every effect's needs, so a new or changed effect is noticed.
- The random draws are the same whatever the limits are. A test checks that "allow everything" equals no generator.

## Interface conventions

- The deck summary breaks effects down by what they give: gold, power, hearts, and other. Each effect counts once per card. Bond and same-type effects count as gold or power based on the card. Other covers draw, exile, discounts, swap, and discard effects.
- Reaping uses a small heart pill on the gold or power stat box. It shows one heart per reaping card in play, displays `♥x4` above three cards, and explains the current heart total in the tooltip.
- Retain uses a regular-weight `↺` line.
- Text selection is disabled in the game area and enabled inside popups so the log can be copied.
- Compact resource values use coloured `●` gold, `⚔` power, and `♥` hearts icons on cards and in the log. Explanatory labels and accessible names keep the resource words.
- The river draws a fixed ring on its fifth slot (`.row[data-edge]::before`, heavy when an enemy is there and light for a recruit), so the ring stays while cards leave and arrive, and lifts with the card on hover. The fifth slot is called the Gate: a label above it, in a heading bar that also has the River name and chevrons pointing right (a CSS mask of `glyphs/chevron.svg`). A lock marker sits on the ring, not on the card, because flush replaces only the four cards to its left. The flush log states that the Gate card cannot be flushed. Player-facing text says "Gate"; code comments and design text still say "right edge" in places.
- The "Your hand" row shows what the cards still in hand would add to gold, power and hearts, so it falls as cards are played and does not move when the player buys. Each card is played on its own on a copy of the current board (`handPreview.ts`), so printed gains and conditional ones (Vault, Hoard, Army and so on) count as the board is now, and the order of the hand does not matter. Swap and Echo count as nothing. Chance and hidden cards stay hidden: a Flip counts at its low amount and cards that a draw brings are not counted, and such a number gets a `+` after it (at least).
- The log uses one copied line per entry instead of paragraph spacing.
- Enemy defeat logs combine resolved direct heart payouts with the base enemy hearts and append any extra gold or power in the same compact resource line.
- The game shows the package version (semver from `package.json`) in the About dialog.

### Look of modes, icons and colour (any theme or skin keeps these)

- A mode has one look, an icon and an accent colour, and it shows the same everywhere: on its card in the challenge picker, on the game header's mode button (icon on the left, ring and tint in the accent), and on the dots, the selected goal and the pressed states of the picker. The looks are one table, `PRESET_LOOKS` in `src/platform/web/challenges.ts`, with one line per preset. A test fails when a preset has no look or its icon file is missing. Adding a mode is a preset in `src/core/custom.ts`, a line in that table, and an icon file.
- Icons are one-colour SVG files in `src/platform/web/glyphs/`, drawn in the text colour with a CSS mask by the `Glyph` component; a new file is picked up by name with no other edit. Multi-colour artwork (coin, swords, heart, retain, lock) lives in `icons/` and is drawn as a background image. Never use a character the font lacks (arrows, ⓘ, ✓, ×, ›): the browser takes it from another font and sizes it differently. Use a glyph, or a plain letter.
- Card art drifts: each shape moves back and forth a few pixels over 3 to 6.5 seconds at the normal setting, with its own direction and start, and entering, focusing or clicking a card gives each shape one push of up to 6 pixels that settles back in about a second, and it plays to its end even when the pointer leaves, through a Web Animation that `artPush.ts` starts on entering (a `:hover` rule would cut the push; CSS-only latches were tried and behaved differently in Chrome and Firefox, or could not restart). Entering again while a push still moves restarts the push from the shape's current place, not from rest and not by finishing the old one (the reach comes from custom properties that `cardArt` writes after all shapes, so the picture itself is unchanged; the drift runs on a group around the shape and the push on the shape, so they do not fight). The animation setting scales the times like the other animations (`loopTimeFactor`: fast is 0.45 times as long, slow is 3 times as long). It stops when the setting is off or the system asks for reduced motion.
- Colours come from the theme variables (`--moss`, `--ember`, `--gold`, `--heart` and so on), never a fixed hex, so light and dark both work. Surfaces are flat: a tint is `color-mix` of the accent into the background, and a ring is a border or an inset shadow. No gradients, shines or drop shadows.
- A goal's result is a star, the hearts and the earliest winning turn on a second, lighter line ("fastest: turn 20") in the same gold when won, `best N ♥` when only played, and "not played" otherwise. The best hearts and the earliest winning turn are two separate records, so a faster win with fewer hearts keeps the old best. The best hearts never go down, the turn never goes up, and a win is never taken away. A run changes only the record of the goal it was started for, however many hearts it ends with. Each entry also stores the game version of the run that last changed it; results are never dropped when the version changes. Stars are drawn shapes, not characters. Settings has Copy results and Paste results: a paste merges by keeping the better record of each pair and refuses anything that is not a results record. The record carries a signature (a salted hash of its entries), stored with it and copied with it. A record whose signature does not match, in the paste box or in storage, is ignored. The source is readable, so this only stops casual edits of a number.
- A run that ends in front of the player opens a "Run summary" dialog: a headline of how it ended, three panels, and the figures. The panels are Stats (a wrapping grid: the run counters and the moves: total, per turn, and the busiest turn) and Hearts. Hearts holds a chart (a bar per finished turn for what it added and a line for the running total, from `stats.heartsAtTurnEnd`) above a bar per heart source (`stats.heartsFrom` in the core: plain recruit hearts, enemy hearts, and each effect by id, with ReapG and ReapP for the end-of-turn reaping). On a narrow dialog the panels sit behind tabs and share one grid cell, so the dialog keeps the height of the tallest and does not jump when the tab changes. On a wide one (a container query) both show side by side and the chart grows to fill the column. When the run improved the record of its goal, gold lines under the headline say so (first result, first win, new best hearts, faster win); they are kept with the address of the run, so a reload shows them again for that run only, and they are dropped when the page opens without that run saved (a new run, or a new game version). The log dialog has a "Run summary…" button that opens it at any time, with "So far" as the headline while the run is in progress. The same facts end the log message as sentences. A run that was already over when the page opened, and a replay, do not open it.
- Screens scale with the width of the play area (container units with a minimum and a maximum), so a wide desktop screen grows type, buttons and rows and does not just stretch them. Buttons in one header row are the same height.
- Scrolling rows use the browser's own scroll-snap, so touch, wheel and trackpad momentum are native. A mouse drag is a bonus on top, and dots jump to an item. The row reports its item when it stops, so a jump does not flicker through the items between. Custom scroll physics is not worth the browser differences.
- Where a screen is, and what it shows, is in the address (the picker's card and goal, the editor's settings), so a reload, a dropped background tab or a link opens the same place. Leaving a screen and coming back keeps its position.
- The compendium (`Compendium.tsx`, view and address in `compendiumView.ts`) is a reference built from the effect registry and the generator's constants, so a new effect shows up with no UI change; a test fails when an effect has no `blurb`. Each effect row ends in slanted bars, one for each colour the effect favours (`lean` and `mildLean` look the same), and the lean filter uses the same colours. It has breadcrumbs instead of a back button, and the tab, the opened effect, the search and the filters are in the address. Its boxes sit in a grid whose tracks stop at 24rem, so a label and its value stay close on a wide screen. The example card of an effect is a generated card chosen by its number (`exampleCard(effect, number)`); tapping the card or the arrow beside it shows the next number, and the other arrow the previous one, so a good example can be found again.
- A warning that is not urgent sits quietly below the action it concerns, not at the top of the screen.
- A render error shows a dialog, and the next hot update (or closing it) reloads the page; the saved run survives. Preact cannot keep the old screen behind it.

## Design boundaries

- Keep the power colour red.
- Keep enemies violet with dashed borders and `Defeat:` effect text.
- Keep Moss, Ember, and Tide as the three gem types.
- Keep the edge-slot outline and the fixed five-card geometry.
- Keep the intentional hand overlap above five cards. Improve focus and semantic state instead of replacing the layout.
- Keep the verbose, copyable log. The pile copy is a block per card (cost, type, one line per effect) and stays as it is: the designer pastes it into chat at the end of a run so the AI can read the small card text.
- Keep pending actions and played-card state clear to assistive technology.
- Do not add new effects before outside players test the current effect set.

The next validation step is to let three to five people play without explanation. Fix problems that several players encounter. Avoid a long polish phase before that test.

## External signals and guardrails

Review research is evidence and inspiration, not a specification. These are things Heartrow should avoid becoming:

- A thin deck should not be the automatic best strategy.
- Mono-colour decks should remain viable without becoming an easy win; cross-colour choices should reward thought.
- Turns should not collapse into “draw a pile, make huge resources, then buy the most expensive cards”.
- A score target can remain the core of the short run, but some players will want modes or objectives with more texture than score attack alone.

Players have also praised the source game's art, polish, and music. Those are quality signals, not a reason to copy its theme, presentation, or card content.

## Rule decisions

Some quirks come from the original prototype. The core keeps them unless listed here as changed.

- **Goal undone by an escape (kept, undecided).** Win is checked when the turn ends, after enemies escape. An enemy that escapes on the last step can still undo a reached goal. See "Win check" in [`ideas.md`](ideas.md).
- **Late filler reroll (kept).** In long mode, from turn 21 onward, a generated card that is only pure gold or power filler is rerolled more often. The turn-21 start was a side effect of an earlier remark, not a tuned value. It is a mode-config field, so it can be moved.
- **Enemies can cost 0 (accepted).** After `weak` or discounts an enemy can cost 0 to defeat. Revisit a minimum cost if play data shows a problem (see "Free enemies" in the ideas).
- **Undo (settled).** Undo is a planning tool, not cheating. The current turn is a reversible workspace and End turn is the commitment boundary. The web session keeps a per-turn stack of snapshots: every accepted move except ending the turn can be undone back to the start of the turn. The RNG streams are part of the state, so a replayed move gives the same random result. Ending the turn clears the stack.
- **Defeat log shows the base cost (kept).** The log line for a defeat shows the printed cost, not the discounted cost paid. Seeing full prices feels good. Revisit if it confuses players.
- **Last-turn refill (kept).** The final `endTurn` still generates one row card before the game ends. It is harmless.

## Bots and balance

- Bots live in `src/core/ai`. A bot is a list of policies. Each policy returns a non-negative factor for every legal move; the factors are multiplied, `0` vetoes a move, and the profiles take the move with the highest product (ties go to the first legal move). The `random` profile has no policies and samples uniformly as a control. These bots give a relative balance signal, not a substitute for human play.
- Policies cover turn order and hand use (`playsFirst`, `retainAware`, `drawFirst`, `effectCardsFirst`, `exileChoices`), row priorities (`buyEnemies`, `buyBuild`, `buyValue`, `noRecruits`, `rush`, `pacifist`, `smartBuy`), flush control (`protectEdge`, `flushWhenStuck`, `smartFlush`), and turn completion (`endTurnWhenDone`). Flush weights belong to the flush policies (`flushWhenStuck`, `smartFlush`, `protectEdge`) and end turn to `endTurnWhenDone`; neither policy sets the other's weight. Both are given the profile's buying policies and count a legal take as something to buy only when none of them vetoes it. Most preference factors use the ordinary 1–10 range; zero remains a deliberate veto, and small factors keep flush and ending the turn as last-resort actions.
- The named profiles are `mixed` (balanced fighter), `none` (no recruits), `build` (early deck building), `rush` (early recruiting only), `pacifist` (no combat and engine cards), and `smart` (unified purchase and flush scoring). `lookahead` remains a separate, greedy rollout experiment.
- Bots select greedily by default. Sampled selection (`--selection sample`) remains available for randomness experiments, but it is a failed experiment and not a bot-balance benchmark.
- The current bot comparison is recorded in `tools/baseline/bots-current.md`. These figures are balance signals, not targets. Earlier measurements and the experiments behind the bots are in [`history.md`](history.md).
- The Long Run bot flushes before defeating an affordable right-edge enemy when another enemy sits to its left, so that left enemy does not become the new escape threat.
- The core was checked against the original prototype over 100 seeds. The generator matches. The bots are 2–4% stronger than the prototype and that was accepted. The report is in `tools/baseline/V7_PARITY.md`.
- Run the simulator with `npm run sim`.

## Making changes

1. Make one focused change.
2. Run `npm run verify`.
3. If generation or effects change, run `npm run sim` for crashes and large balance changes, in both modes.
4. Test the page in a browser at about 360 px and 760 px wide.
5. Check the row, seven-card hands, popups, flushes, Retain, exile, and undo.
6. Record a design decision here before changing the rules.
